# cta-footer-reveal

A close on a non-contrasting surface (a split).

## Purpose

The quiet close: a final ask carried on a calm, non-contrasting surface — often a split of message
and action — that eases the page into its footer. It solves the pushiness problem: when a loud,
high-contrast closing band would feel like a hard sell, this settles the ask into the page's own tone
so the invitation lands as a natural continuation rather than a jolt.

## Choose when

- The brand or page tone is understated and a loud contrasting close would jar.
- The ending should flow into the footer without a hard visual break — a reveal, not a punctuation
  mark.
- The audience is quietly ready and responds better to a calm invitation than to pressure.
- A split of message and action reads more gracefully here than a centred banner.

## Avoid when

- The close needs to lift off the page and command the moment — reach for a contrasting banner or a
  decorated flagship close.
- The strongest ending offers onward links for a hand-off — reach for the close with link columns.
- The next step is really to hand over information — reach for a contact or lead-capture role.

## Character

Calm and continuous — low conversion pressure by design, trading a contrasting lift for a smooth
transition. The ask sits within the page's own surface, balanced across a split rather than shouted
from a band. It is read at the close and offers a single action, quietly presented.

## Composition

It comes at the foot of the page and reveals the footer directly beneath it, following the last body
beat. It should not sit beside another call-to-action, nor be forced next to a high-contrast band that
would break the calm transition it exists to provide.

## Contract

Assumes the page has already earned belief and that the ending suits restraint over spectacle. It
expects one clear action and, at most, a line of supporting copy; it borrows all its credibility from
what precedes it and adds no new argument of its own.

## Judgement

Used well, it lets a quietly-argued page settle into its footer with one calm, self-assured ask. The
tempting misuse is choosing it when the page actually needs a commanding finale, so the close passes
unnoticed — or dressing it up until it stops being the calm reveal it was for. Made loud it loses its
only advantage; placed before the argument is won it asks too soon and converts poorly.
