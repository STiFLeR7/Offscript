# get-started

Lighter closing-half process; leads into the ask.

## Purpose

The lighter closing-half process: a short, ordered run of steps whose job is not to teach the whole
method but to hand a readier reader into the ask. It shows just enough of the path — a few plain,
sequential steps — to make starting feel easy, then leads directly into the call to action.

## Choose when

- The process is a simple lead-in to the ask, not the page's main feasibility argument.
- A few light, ordered steps are enough to make beginning feel low-effort.
- The reader is already fairly convinced and needs the first move made obvious.
- It belongs late on the page, folding the sequence into the next action.

## Avoid when

- The steps are substantial and reward dwelling — reach for the canonical multi-step walk.
- The items are parallel rather than sequential — reach for a feature / value-prop role.
- Feasibility is still genuinely in doubt and needs the fuller, heavier treatment.
- There is no real next action for the sequence to lead into.

## Character

Brisk and encouraging: an ordered but unweighted sequence that reads quickly and points forward.
Lighter than the canonical walk, it keeps the family's method — steps in true order, each opening onto
the next — but compresses it, existing to lower the barrier to the ask rather than to prove the whole
path.

## Composition

It sits late on the page, near or as the closing move, a compact ordered sequence that resolves into the
call to action rather than standing alone. The steps stay genuinely sequential and self-evident in
order, and the final one opens directly onto the ask instead of closing the journey and inviting the
reader to stop.

## Contract

Assumes a short, real sequence that leads into a concrete next action. It expects the steps to be
honestly ordered and light, and the ask to actually follow; imposed order on parallel items still
deceives here, and a closing-half process with nothing to lead into is a sequence that goes nowhere.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader who is nearly persuaded sees how simple starting is and moves straight into the ask.
The tempting misuse is loading it with substantial steps it cannot carry — that is the canonical walk's
job — or letting it trail off without the action it exists to set up. It is the family's lead-in shape:
keep it light, keep it ordered, and keep it pointed at the next step.
