# hero-bento

Product hero with a cluster of real proof tiles.

## Purpose

The opening screen for a product whose credibility is best argued by showing several true
outcomes at once. It solves the opening-trust problem: a visitor who does not yet believe the
promise is shown a constellation of concrete proof — numbers, named results, recognizable marks —
in the same glance as the headline, so the claim and its evidence arrive together.

## Choose when

- The strongest argument is breadth of proof: several outcomes that are individually modest but
  collectively convincing.
- The audience is evaluative and proof-driven — buyers comparing options, not casual browsers.
- Early in the journey, atop a page that will go on to substantiate each proof in turn.
- The brand can stand on a confident, established trust level because real proof exists to show.

## Avoid when

- The hero's real job is to capture intent or start a conversation — reach for a
  conversational, lead-capturing hero instead.
- The product is pre-proof (a launch or a vision) with no genuine numbers — a tile cluster filled
  with invented figures reads as hollow; choose a promise-led or narrative hero.
- The page is short and quiet, where a dense proof grid would overpower the layout.

## Character

A high-density, confident opening: assertive rather than calm, signalling "much is true here."
The proof reads as a structured cluster that shares the stage with the headline rather than
sitting beneath it. It is read, not operated — there is no interaction to perform.

## Composition

It opens the page, so little precedes it. It hands off naturally to a feature or how-it-works
section that expands each promised proof, leading later into deeper social proof. It should not
sit beside another hero, nor be followed immediately by a second heavy proof band that repeats
the same beat.

## Contract

Assumes several genuine proof points already exist — real metrics, named outcomes, or
recognizable marks. It expects a handful of concrete, true figures or marks to be supplied;
without them the tiles are empty frames.

## Judgement

Used well, a product with real traction opens with a few honest proof tiles beside a sharp
promise. The tempting misuse is padding the cluster with vanity or invented numbers to fill the
grid, or choosing it when there is one story to tell rather than many proofs to show. When the
evidence is thin or fabricated it reads as filler and erodes the very trust a hero must build;
over-dense, it buries the headline it was meant to support.
