# outcome-stats

Outcome argument with meters; numbers need narrative.

## Purpose

Frames figures as outcomes, pairing each number with the narrative and progress it represents so
the reader understands not just the value but what it means. It solves the bare-number problem:
when a metric needs context to be persuasive — a before-and-after, a trajectory, a result tied to a
claim — a plain figure under-sells it, so this band gives the numbers their story.

## Choose when

- The numbers only persuade once their meaning is explained — they are outcomes, not bare stats.
- A result is best shown as movement or progress toward a goal rather than a single static figure.
- The argument is "here is the change we produced", and the narrative is part of the proof.
- The reader needs the context around a metric to grant it weight.

## Avoid when

- The figures speak for themselves and need no story — reach for a plainer card or row treatment.
- There is no real outcome or trajectory to narrate — manufactured progress reads as spin.
- A human account would tell the outcome more convincingly — reach for a case-study or testimonial
  role.

## Character

A measured, explanatory band of medium density and steady, narrative energy: figures presented as
outcomes, each carrying a short story or a sense of progress. It reads as a reasoned argument from
evidence rather than a quick proof flash, and the numbers are framed, not merely displayed.

## Composition

It sits mid-page where a claim needs its result demonstrated, after the value has been introduced,
and hands off toward deeper proof or the ask. It pairs poorly directly beside another dense number
band — two narrated proof sections in a row overload the reader.

## Contract

Assumes real, current, verifiable outcomes that genuinely have a narrative — a change, a
trajectory, or a result tied to a claim — and the copy to explain them. It expects each figure to
be backed by the story it is given, not dressed in a narrative it has not earned.

## Judgement

Used well, a meaningful result is shown as the outcome it is, the numbers made legible by the story
around them. The tempting misuse is wrapping ordinary figures in invented narrative to inflate
them, or using meters where a flat number would do. When the story outruns the evidence, the band
reads as spin and the outcome loses its credibility.
