# testimonial-stack

Quote plus metrics for one named customer.

## Purpose

The family's anchor shape: a single named customer's words paired with the results they earned. It
answers the "does this actually work for someone like me?" doubt with the most direct evidence the
family offers — one real voice, attributed, with concrete outcomes beside the quote, so belief and
proof land together on one credible card.

## Choose when

- One named customer's story, told compactly, is the most persuasive proof available.
- A quote reads far stronger when a hard result sits next to it.
- Near the decision, beside the claim or ask the testimony supports.
- A single, focused piece of lived proof fits better than breadth or volume.

## Avoid when

- Breadth is the argument — many voices matter more than one — reach for a multi-voice set.
- The proof is really a long narrative that needs room to unfold — reach for a deep single story.
- The proof is a mark rather than a voice — reach for a social-proof role — or a bare number — reach
  for a stats / outcomes role.

## Character

Focused and credible, quiet in energy but high in trust: one voice, one face, one result, presented
without spectacle. It is read for resonance rather than operated — a still card that lets the
customer's own words and numbers carry the weight.

## Composition

It sits in the body beside the claim it vouches for, and reads well after a feature or value beat that
made a promise this customer confirms. It should not be stranded far from the claim it supports, nor
stacked against a second heavy proof band that repeats the same beat.

## Contract

Assumes a real, permitted quote from a named customer with a genuine, attributable result. It expects
the voice, the attribution, and at least one concrete outcome to be real; a fabricated, composite, or
embellished customer collapses the proof entirely, and a vague quote with no result persuades no one.

## Judgement

Used well, one named customer says — in their own words and with a real number — that this delivered.
The tempting misuse is inventing or polishing the customer, or leaning on a glowing but unspecific
quote with nothing concrete beside it. Fabricated or embellished, it destroys the trust the whole
family exists to build; vague or number-less, it reads as decoration rather than proof.
