# team-visionaries

Minimal founders row for a small team.

## Purpose

The minimal founders row: a spare presentation of the few people who founded or drive a small
organization, named with their roles. It exists to make a small team's credibility personal — a
founder-scale introduction where intimacy and directness, not breadth, are what convince a reader that
real, committed people stand behind the offering.

## Choose when

- The organization is small and its credibility is carried by its founders.
- A few named people, shown plainly, are more convincing than a large grid would be.
- The reader responds to a personal, founder-scale introduction.
- Intimacy and directness suit the stage and size of the company.

## Avoid when

- The team is broad and breadth is the argument — reach for the portrait grid.
- The credibility rests on senior executive accountability — reach for the formal leadership row.
- The people need to be paired with substantial narrative — reach for the team-plus-story hybrid.
- The people are not part of the credibility argument — reach for another proof role.

## Character

Personal and understated: a minimal row of a few real founders, presented plainly. It is the family's
lightest shape — a handful of people, close and direct — and stays static and honest, trading the
reassurance of numbers for the credibility of naming the actual people who started and steer the work.

## Composition

It sits where a small team's people are the argument, as a spare row of founders with names and roles,
supporting the ask rather than making it. It should stay minimal and genuine — a few real people, not
padded toward a grid — and never manufacture scale a small team does not have.

## Contract

Assumes a small number of real founders or principals, named with true roles and shown with permission.
It expects every person to be genuine and every role real; inventing founders, or padding the row to
seem larger than the team is, breaks the intimate honesty that is this shape's whole appeal.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader meets the few real people who drive a small organization and trusts it as personal
and committed. The tempting misuse is inflating a founders row toward a fuller team it does not have, or
using it where executive authority or breadth would carry more weight. Its power is honest intimacy —
the small scale is the point, and faking size forfeits it.
