# value-prop

Minimal number-progression; a quick proof beat between heavier bands.

## Purpose

Provides a light, minimal proof beat that punctuates the page between heavier sections without
asking much of the reader. It solves the unrelenting-density problem: when several substantial
bands run together, a brief value beat gives the reader a breath while still advancing the
argument with a quick, honest proof point.

## Choose when

- The page needs a short, low-effort beat to break up a run of heavier bands.
- A couple of quick proof points can advance the argument without a full section.
- A change of pace — a brief rest with substance — serves the rhythm of the page.
- The surrounding bands are dense enough that an interlude earns its place.

## Avoid when

- The section must carry the main value argument — it is too light; reach for a fuller feature or
  value band.
- It would stand alone with nothing heavier around it to punctuate — a lone light beat reads as
  thin.
- There is no quick proof to offer — an empty interlude wastes a band.

## Character

A minimal, light, low-density band: a brief breath in the page's rhythm rather than a full
argument. The energy is quiet and quick, a palate-cleanser between weightier sections, read in a
moment and not operated.

## Composition

It belongs between heavier bands, as a deliberate change of pace, and never as the page's main
feature section. It works best flanked by denser sections it can punctuate, and loses its purpose
if several light beats run together.

## Contract

Assumes a couple of quick, genuine proof points and heavier bands around it to give the interlude
meaning. It expects to be brief — its value is the pause it provides, so it must not try to do a
full section's work.

## Judgement

Used well, the page breathes between its substantial sections without losing momentum. The
tempting misuse is asking this light beat to carry an argument it was never sized for. When it is
made to do a heavy band's job, the page feels thin exactly where the reader expected substance.
