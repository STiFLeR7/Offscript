# results-proof

Proof hybrid: one story anchoring a few numbers.

## Purpose

The proof hybrid: a row of trust marks with one anchoring quote or metric folded in, so a single
concrete result gives the ambient signal something to hold onto. It is still social proof — marks lead
and credibility is borrowed by association — but it lets one real customer's figure or line steady the
row, for the reader who wants a foothold, not just familiar names.

## Choose when

- Marks alone feel too weightless, and one concrete anchor would make the trust land harder.
- A single real customer result — a figure or a short line — can stand for the rest without becoming
  the focus.
- Borrowed credibility still leads, and the anchor is there to steady it, not to carry the section.
- The page wants proof with a little more substance than a bare strip but less weight than a full case.

## Avoid when

- Marks alone already do the job — reach for the early strip or the contrasting trust beat.
- The customer's voice is what truly persuades — reach for a testimonials role and let the quote lead.
- The numbers are the argument — reach for a stats / outcomes role and give them the stage.
- Several stories deserve room — reach for a case or story shape rather than one folded anchor.

## Character

Ambient but anchored: a trust row given one point of gravity. It is the most substantial member of its
family yet still support, not spotlight — the marks set the tone while the single anchor keeps a
sceptical eye from sliding off. It hybridises without tipping over: were the voice or the number to take
the lead, it would no longer belong here.

## Composition

It sits in the body where a plain mark-row would feel thin, pairing the marks with exactly one anchoring
quote or figure and no more. The anchor must stay subordinate — a steadying detail beside the marks —
and the section must not swell into a testimonial block or a metrics band, which are other families'
work.

## Contract

Assumes real, permitted marks plus one genuine, attributable result — a true figure or a real customer
line, used with permission. It expects the anchor to be real, specific, and subordinate to the marks; an
invented figure, an unattributed quote, or an anchor that quietly becomes the whole point breaks both
the trust and the boundary that keeps this shape social proof.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader gets familiar names and one concrete reason to believe them, and moves on convinced
without being detained. The misuse is fabricating the anchor, or letting the single figure or line grow
until the section is really a testimonial or a stats band wearing logos. Its discipline is restraint:
one real anchor, marks still leading — cross that line and it stops being what it is.
