# team-leadership

Formal/executive leadership row.

## Purpose

The formal leadership row: a deliberate presentation of the executives who lead the organization, named
with their roles. Where the grid shows breadth, this shows authority — it answers "who is accountable at
the top" by putting the decision-makers, few and senior, on the record with their responsibilities.

## Choose when

- The credibility argument rests on the seniority and accountability of named executives.
- A small number of leaders carry more weight than a broad team would.
- The reader wants to know who is ultimately responsible for the offering.
- A formal, composed row suits the weight of the people it presents.

## Avoid when

- Breadth of the whole team is the point — reach for the portrait grid.
- The organization is a small founding team with a story — reach for the minimal founders row.
- People need to be paired with narrative on a richer page — reach for the team-plus-story hybrid.
- The people are not part of the credibility argument — reach for another proof role.

## Character

Formal and authoritative: a composed row of senior figures, presented with the gravity their roles
carry. It is the family's most weighted shape — fewer people, each more significant — and stays static
and factual, letting real names and real titles establish who stands accountable without embellishment.

## Composition

It sits where leadership credibility is being made, as a formal row of executives with names and roles,
supporting the ask rather than making it. It should carry only genuine, senior figures with accurate
titles, stay a deliberate row rather than a sprawling grid, and never inflate seniority to borrow
authority that was not earned.

## Contract

Assumes real executives with accurate names and true titles, shown with permission. It expects every
role to be genuine and current; an inflated title, a figurehead who does not actually lead, or a
fabricated executive is the gravest breach here — this shape trades on accountability, so a false claim
of who leads is unrecoverable.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader learns exactly who is accountable at the top and extends trust to a named, senior
team. The tempting misuse is dressing up junior or nominal figures as leadership, or leaning on a formal
row when breadth or a founder story would persuade more. Its authority is real accountability — claim
leadership that is not real and the row discredits the page.
