# pricing-packages

Unequal offers/packages of differing scope.

## Purpose

The package set: an unequal group of offers or packages of differing scope — not parallel tiers of one
thing, but distinct bundles of different size and shape. It makes the cost of each legible when the
offering is sold as services or packages that are not directly comparable peers, so a reader can find
the scope that matches their need.

## Choose when

- The offers differ in scope — services or bundles of different size — rather than being parallel peers.
- A reader must match their need to a package, not pick a tier on a common ladder.
- The distinct shapes of each offer are part of what the reader is choosing between.
- Cost is part of the decision and each package's price and contents must be clear.

## Avoid when

- The plans are genuine parallel peers of one product — reach for the equal plan cards.
- Plans divide by audience or persona — reach for the persona-split tabbed set.
- The page is short and price must fold in reassurance — reach for the pricing-plus-FAQ band.
- The question is feature parity between comparable plans — reach for a comparison role.

## Character

Transparent and scope-led: a set of unequal packages presented so their differences in size and shape
are honest and legible, not forced into a false equivalence. It shares the family's low-anxiety
candour — clear cost, no pressure — but arranges offers by genuine difference rather than as ranked
peers.

## Composition

It sits late, near the decision, as the page's single pricing component, presenting packages of
differing scope with their real contents and prices. It must not pretend unequal offers are equal peers,
must remain the only price table on the page, and should let each package's true scope show rather than
flattening them into a tier ladder.

## Contract

Assumes real packages of genuinely differing scope, each with a true price and honest contents. It
expects the inequality to be real and clearly shown — misrepresenting scope, hiding what a package
excludes, or dressing pressure as choice breaks the transparency the family requires; and it, too, must
be the only pricing component on the page.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader with a specific need finds the package whose scope fits and knows exactly what it
costs. The tempting misuse is blurring real differences to steer the reader, or hiding what a cheaper
package leaves out. Its value is honest scope — show the packages as they truly differ, and let the
reader choose without pressure.
