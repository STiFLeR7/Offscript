# feature-stack

Cinematic pinned sequence; long pages only, once per page.

## Purpose

Turns a sequence of features into a single, dwelt-on, cinematic passage that holds the reader in
one place while the story advances. It solves the flat-scroll problem for a flagship narrative:
when one feature arc deserves real attention, a pinned, unfolding treatment makes the page slow
down and the argument land with weight.

## Choose when

- One feature story is important enough to earn an immersive, slowed-down moment.
- The page is long enough to host a heavy, attention-claiming passage without starving the rest.
- The sequence genuinely rewards dwelling — each beat builds on the last.
- The audience is engaged and mid-journey, willing to be carried through a longer arc.

## Avoid when

- The page is short — a heavy pinned passage overwhelms a brief page; reach for a plain feature
  band instead.
- More than one such moment is wanted on a page — a second cinematic passage competes with the
  first and both lose their gravity.
- The content is a quick scan, not a story — an even grid or trio respects the reader's time.

## Character

A high-energy, immersive, directed band: the page's single most cinematic feature moment, holding
focus while the narrative advances. Density is high but staged, revealed a beat at a time, and the
intent is to be experienced, not merely scanned.

## Composition

It lives deep inside a long page, once, as the flagship feature passage, with calmer bands before
and after to give it room. It should not sit beside another heavy, attention-claiming moment, and
never carries a short page on its own.

## Contract

Assumes a long page with enough surrounding bands to justify a heavy passage, and a feature
sequence whose beats genuinely build on one another. It expects real narrative payoff at the end —
not motion for its own sake.

## Judgement

Used well, a signature capability gets one memorable, unhurried passage that the rest of the page
frames. The tempting misuse is reaching for the drama on a short page, or using it more than once.
When the motion has no story behind it, the passage reads as a gimmick and spends the reader's
patience for nothing.
