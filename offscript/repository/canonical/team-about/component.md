# team-about

Team plus story hybrid; richer about pages.

## Purpose

The team-plus-story hybrid: real people paired with narrative copy, for a richer about page where
who-we-are and why-we-do-this are told together. It goes beyond portraits and roles to fold in the
organization's story, so the reader meets both the faces behind the offering and the account of what
they stand for.

## Choose when

- The about page can afford narrative, and people plus story together persuade more than either alone.
- The organization's story is part of its credibility, not just its team roster.
- The reader is on a richer page and will read closely where story copy is present.
- People and narrative reinforce each other rather than competing for the same beat.

## Avoid when

- Only breadth of the team is needed — reach for the portrait grid.
- The argument is senior accountability — reach for the formal leadership row.
- A small team's founders alone carry it — reach for the minimal founders row.
- The people are not part of the credibility argument — reach for another proof role.

## Character

Human and narrative, the richest member of its family: portraits given the company of real story copy,
read more closely than a grid is scanned. It is warmer and more expansive than the other shapes, yet
stays honest and specific — the story earns its length only if the people and the account behind it are
real.

## Composition

It sits on richer about or company pages where there is room for both people and narrative, pairing
genuine portraits and roles with story copy, and supports the ask rather than making it. The people and
the story must reinforce one another; it should not swell into an untethered essay, nor bury real
portraits under copy that outstays the substance behind it.

## Contract

Assumes genuine team members with real names and roles plus a true organizational story. It expects both
halves to be real: invented faces or fabricated roles fail as in any team shape, and a story embellished
beyond what the people and the work support hollows out the very narrative that justifies this hybrid.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader on an about page meets real people and the genuine story behind them, and trusts the
whole more than portraits or prose alone. The tempting misuse is spinning a story richer than the truth,
or reaching for the hybrid where a plain grid or a leadership row would have sufficed. Its credibility
is doubled and so is its exposure — both the faces and the story must be real.
