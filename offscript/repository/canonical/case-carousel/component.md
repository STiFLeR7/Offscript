# case-carousel

Editorial case studies; a heavier band.

## Purpose

The editorial shape: a browsable set of case studies, each a heavier, structured account of a
customer's problem, work, and result. It answers the "show me the whole story, not just a quote" need
for evaluative readers — offering several deeper, editorial proofs a reader can move between, where
each stands as a substantial argument on its own.

## Choose when

- The proof needs depth and structure — problem, approach, outcome — not a single line.
- Several such case studies exist and letting the reader browse between them adds value.
- The audience is evaluative and rewards substance: buyers doing real diligence.
- A heavier proof band is warranted by the weight of the decision.

## Avoid when

- A short quote or a compact quote-plus-metrics card carries enough — reach for those lighter shapes.
- The signal is volume of brief praise — reach for a review carousel.
- Only one story matters and deserves the full page — reach for a deep single story.
- The proof is a mark — reach for a social-proof role — or a number — reach for a stats / outcomes
  role.

## Character

Substantial and editorial, the densest testimonials shape: each entry a structured account rather than
a snippet, browsed one at a time. It reads as considered journalism about real outcomes; its weight is
the point, so it earns more room and attention than a quote card.

## Composition

It sits deeper in the body, often where a page turns to serious evidence, and can lead toward
resources or the closing ask. It is a heavy band and should not crowd another heavy proof section, nor
be asked to open a page before lighter orientation has been given.

## Contract

Assumes several real, permitted case studies with genuine problems, work, and attributable outcomes.
It expects each account to be real and substantive; a set of thin or embellished "cases" with no real
outcome betrays the depth it promises and wastes the reader's diligence.

## Judgement

Used well, an evaluative reader browses several genuine, structured stories of customers who succeeded.
The tempting misuse is dressing up shallow blurbs as case studies, or choosing this heavy band when a
quote would have sufficed. Without real substance behind each entry it reads as marketing in a serious
costume; misplaced, its weight stalls a page that needed momentum.
