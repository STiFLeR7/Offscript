# backed-by

Investor/credibility wall; never invented names.

## Purpose

The credibility wall: a deliberate, assembled block of investor, partner, or backer marks that answers
who stands behind the offering. Where the early strip whispers in passing, this states — on the pages
where the people and institutions behind the work are themselves part of the argument — that serious,
nameable backers have already committed.

## Choose when

- The backers are part of the credibility case, and naming them changes how the offering is judged.
- The page is an about or company page, where who-stands-behind matters as much as what-is-sold.
- The marks are weighty enough — investors, notable partners — that assembling them deliberately is
  warranted.
- A considered, standing statement of backing fits better than a light passing strip.

## Avoid when

- The moment calls for a quick, early glance of trust — reach for the strip under the hero.
- Breadth of many passing marks is the point — reach for the contrasting trust beat.
- One customer story should anchor a few numbers — reach for the proof-hybrid shape.
- The proof is a voice — reach for a testimonials role — or a bare number — reach for a stats /
  outcomes role.

## Character

Deliberate and grounded: a composed wall that reads as a considered statement rather than a passing
beat. Heavier and more settled than the early strip, it still stays ambient — it establishes standing,
it does not argue or plead. Its weight is in whose names it carries, not in how loudly it carries them.

## Composition

It sits where the credibility of the backers is being made — typically an about or company page — as a
settled block the reader can study rather than glance past. It should carry only genuinely weighty
marks, remain a supporting statement rather than the page's headline, and never dilute strong backers by
padding the wall.

## Contract

Assumes real, permitted backer and investor marks whose association is true and current. It expects
every name to be genuine and every relationship real and permitted; an invented backer, a lapsed
relationship shown as live, or an implied endorsement that was never given is the family's gravest
breach here — this shape names names, so a false one is unrecoverable.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader weighing whether to take the offering seriously sees exactly who already has, and
extends that trust forward. The tempting misuse is inflating the wall with tenuous or expired
associations, or letting a list of backers stand in for the argument the page still owes. Credibility
here is borrowed from real institutions; borrow a name that was never given and the whole wall — and the
page behind it — is discredited.
