# hero-horizon-light

A gentle mid-page or closing invitation (a centered close, not a hero).

## Purpose

The gentlest invitation: a light, centred ask that can sit between bands mid-page or close a page
softly. It solves the low-pressure-nudge problem — when a full closing band would be too much but the
reader could use a quiet prompt to act, it offers one centred, unhurried invitation without the weight
of a terminal close. Despite its name it is a call-to-action, not an opening hero.

## Choose when

- A soft, optional nudge fits between sections, offering the next step without interrupting the flow.
- The page wants a light close rather than a heavy, contrasting finale.
- The audience is only partway to a decision and a gentle, centred invitation suits better than
  pressure.
- One quiet, self-contained ask is all the moment needs.

## Avoid when

- The page has fully argued its case and deserves a confident terminal close — reach for a banner, a
  link-column band, or a decorated flagship close.
- It would be mistaken for the opening screen — it is never the page's hero, only a mid-page or
  closing invitation.
- The ending needs onward links for a hand-off, or a memorable flagship finale — reach for those
  richer closes instead.

## Character

Light and unhurried — the lowest conversion pressure in the family, a centred invitation on a calm
surface rather than a commanding band. Its energy is gentle by design; it invites without insisting.
It carries a single action, presented quietly and centred.

## Composition

It can appear between content bands mid-page, or as a soft close near the end; either way it needs calm
on both sides. It should not sit beside another call-to-action, nor open the page as if it were a hero,
nor crowd a heavier close that would overpower its gentleness.

## Contract

Assumes a moment where a light touch converts better than pressure, and that a single low-cost action
is worth offering here. It expects one clear, gentle action and little else; it adds no new argument
and leans on the surrounding content to make the invitation feel timely.

## Judgement

Used well, it drops a quiet, well-placed invitation into the middle of a page, or ends one softly,
without ever raising its voice. The tempting misuse is treating it as a hero at the top of the page,
or leaning on it as the sole close where a firmer ask was needed. Mistaken for an opening it orients no
one; asked to carry the final conversion alone it under-closes a page that had earned a stronger
finish.
