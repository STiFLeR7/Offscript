# resources-carousel

Sliding; more items than a static band holds.

## Purpose

The sliding overflow variant: a carousel for when there are more real items to surface than any static
band can hold at once. It keeps the family's evidence-of-substance job while letting a larger set live in
a single band, sliding the extra items into reach so a reader can browse a longer shelf without the page
paying for every card in vertical space.

## Choose when

- There are more genuine, current items than a static grid or row can show at once.
- The full set is worth offering, but showing it all statically would overwhelm the band's space.
- A reader benefits from browsing a longer sequence at their own pace, sliding through it.
- Motion here is wayfinding through overflow, not decoration — the count truly exceeds a static layout.

## Avoid when

- The items fit comfortably in a static grid or row — a carousel then hides content behind interaction for no reason.
- There is not enough real material to justify sliding — padding a carousel to fill it is worse than a smaller static band.
- Every item must be visible at a glance — sliding defers most of them out of view by design.
- The material is specifically third-party press best read as a compact row — reach for the news row.

## Character

Substantive but space-thrifty: the family's overflow shape, trading all-at-once visibility for capacity.
It still offers paths rather than forcing them and still reads as evidence of a living offering, but it
accepts that most items sit just out of view until slid to — a browsable sequence, not a wall, and never
motion for its own sake.

## Composition

It is a sliding sequence of resource cards, each a real, current item with a resolving link, holding more
than a static band would. The sliding must serve genuine overflow, not conceal a thin set behind
interaction; the visible frame must still read as substantive on its own, and every card — seen
immediately or only after sliding — must be real and resolve.

## Contract

Assumes a genuinely larger set of real items than a static layout holds, and a reader willing to slide
through it. It expects the carousel to earn its motion with true overflow and every item to be genuine
with a working link; a carousel padded to look full, or built to disguise a handful of cards as many,
betrays both the family's substance rule and the reader's effort to browse.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader browses a long, credible shelf of real resources at their own pace, taking in the
breadth without the page spending endless vertical space. The tempting misuse is reaching for the slide
when a static band would show everything better, or padding the track to seem well-stocked — interaction
that costs the reader and returns nothing. Its value is honest capacity: real overflow made browsable.
