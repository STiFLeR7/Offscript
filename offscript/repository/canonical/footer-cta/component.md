# footer-cta

CTA-fused footer; one-band close on short pages.

## Purpose

The one-band close: a footer that folds a final action into the wayfinding so a short page ends in a
single band rather than a separate closing call-to-action stacked above a separate footer. It exists for
pages too brief to earn two distinct ending bands — it lets the page make its last ask and hand over its
map in one economical move, so a short page closes cleanly instead of trailing through redundant
structure.

## Choose when

- The page is short and does not warrant a full closing band and a separate footer beneath it.
- The final ask and the wayfinding can share one band without either feeling cramped.
- Economy of structure serves a brief page better than a formal two-part ending.
- The close needs a single, modest call to action integrated with the way onward.

## Avoid when

- The page has earned a distinct, commanding closing band above its footer — keep them separate.
- The close should carry brand voice and breadth — reach for the editorial mega-footer.
- The ending's job is a capture rather than an action — reach for the footer with a built-in form.
- Orientation is needed at the top of the page, not the bottom — that is the navigation role.

## Character

Economical and integrated: the family's most compact working shape, fusing a final action into the
closing frame so a short page needs only one band to end. It stays a footer at heart — the action it
carries is modest and subordinate, sharing the band with the wayfinding rather than shouting over it —
and reads as a tidy, single close rather than a loud finale.

## Composition

It is a single closing band that combines a modest call to action with the footer's destination links,
brand mark, and utility row. The action must stay subordinate to the page's primary ask and never rival
it; the wayfinding must remain complete despite the shared band. Its footer half must stay consistent
with the site's other footers even as the CTA half varies by page.

## Contract

Assumes a short page where a separate closing band would be redundant, and a final action modest enough
to share the footer's band. It expects the fusion to stay balanced — real wayfinding, a subordinate ask
— not a closing banner wearing a footer's links; a CTA that overpowers the frame, or a footer half that
drifts page to page, breaks the economy this variant exists to provide.

## Judgement

Used well, a brief page ends in one clean band that both asks once and points the way onward, sparing
the reader redundant closing structure. The tempting misuse is reaching for it on a page that deserved a
full, distinct finale, so the ending feels undersized — or letting the fused action swell until the
footer stops being a footer. Its value is an economical single close for pages that genuinely need only
one.
