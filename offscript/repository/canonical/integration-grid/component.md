# integration-grid

Each connection needs a line of copy.

## Purpose

The explained integration grid: a grid of connection marks where each one carries a line of copy saying
what it does or how it fits. It answers "does this work with what I already have?" for stacks where the
connections are not self-evident — each mark earning its place by explaining the specific fit, so a
reader can find their tool and see exactly how it connects.

## Choose when

- Ecosystem fit is part of the decision and each connection benefits from a line of explanation.
- The connections are not self-evident — the reader needs to know what each one actually does.
- The stack matters enough that a considered, per-connection grid is warranted.
- A reader is weighing adoption and asking whether their existing tools are supported.

## Avoid when

- The marks speak for themselves and need no copy — reach for the lighter marquee.
- Only a quiet passing beat is wanted, not a considered grid.
- The marks signal trust rather than compatibility — reach for a social-proof role.
- There is nothing real to explain per connection — a grid that argues nothing is decoration.

## Character

Practical and explanatory: a grid of real connections, each paired with a line that makes its fit
concrete. It is the heavier of the two integration shapes — it dwells, one line per mark — and stays
reassuring rather than promotional, letting the specifics of genuine support do the arguing.

## Composition

It sits mid-page, where adoption is being weighed, as a grid of connection marks each with a short line
of copy. Every entry must be a real, supported connection the reader could rely on; the copy must
explain genuine fit, not pad the grid, and it should not swell past the connections that actually matter
to the decision.

## Contract

Assumes real, supported connections, each with something true to say about how it fits. It expects every
mark and its line to be honest — never implying an integration that does not exist or overstating a
partial one; a grid whose copy argues nothing, or claims a fit that is not real, becomes decoration and
does not belong.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader finds their tools in the grid and reads exactly how each connects, and "will it fit
my stack?" stops being a silent objection. The tempting misuse is padding the grid with connections that
do not really exist or explain nothing, or claiming support that is only aspirational. Its value is
concrete, honest fit — each line must be real, or the grid is worse than empty.
