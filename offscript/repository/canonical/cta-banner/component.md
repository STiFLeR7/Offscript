# cta-banner

Shortest close; when a footer follows immediately.

## Purpose

The most compact final ask: a single, high-contrast invitation to act, placed where the page is
ready to end. It solves the long-goodbye problem — when the argument is already made, a heavy
closing section adds friction; this band makes the ask once, cleanly, and gets out of the way.

## Choose when

- The page's case is complete and only the ask remains.
- A footer follows immediately, so the close should be brief rather than a destination in itself.
- The audience is decided or nearly so — late journey, after the proof has landed.
- A confident, single-action moment suits the brand.

## Avoid when

- The reader still needs reassurance or options before deciding — use a richer close that carries
  supporting detail.
- It would sit mid-page with more content to follow: a terminal-feeling band mid-flow signals a
  false ending.
- A flagship, decorated finale is wanted — reach for a more expressive closing treatment used
  once per page.

## Character

Minimal and decisive: one message, one action. It reads as a clean punctuation mark rather than a
crescendo, lifted off the page by its contrasting surface to signal "this is the moment." Its only
interaction is the single primary action it offers.

## Composition

It hands off immediately to the footer below it and follows the page's final proof or feature
beat. It should not sit beside another call-to-action — competing asks dilute the close — nor
beside a second contrasting band that would flatten the lift it depends on.

## Contract

Assumes the surrounding page has already earned the ask; the banner itself carries no new
argument. It expects nothing beyond a clear single action and its label, borrowing all its
credibility from what precedes it.

## Judgement

Used well, it ends a fully-argued page with one confident invitation, immediately above the
footer. The tempting misuse is leaning on it as the only persuasion — a bare ask with nothing
earned before it — or stacking several asks together. With no preceding argument it feels abrupt
and converts poorly; repeated or placed mid-page it creates false endings and decision fatigue.
