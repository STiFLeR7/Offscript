# mission-reveal

One manifesto-grade statement; used once, max.

## Purpose

Gives a single, manifesto-grade statement the whole band to itself, so one conviction lands with
full force. It solves the buried-belief problem: when a page has one idea big enough to define it,
crowding that idea among features dilutes it — a spacious, singular reveal lets the statement carry
the weight no list could.

## Choose when

- There is exactly one idea genuinely worth a manifesto-scale moment.
- The page can spare a band that does nothing but state a single conviction with air around it.
- A declarative pause will reframe how the reader reads everything after it.
- The statement is strong enough that stripping everything else away makes it stronger.

## Avoid when

- There are several ideas to convey — reach for a feature band; a manifesto holds one thought only.
- It would appear more than once on a page — a second reveal cancels the gravity of the first.
- The statement is ordinary — inflating a routine line to manifesto scale exposes how little it
  says.

## Character

A bold, singular, high-conviction band of deliberately low density and generous space: one
statement, framed by air. The energy is declarative and assured, a pause that asks to be read
slowly, never a section to be operated or scanned.

## Composition

It works as a standalone manifesto moment — often high on the page to set the tone, or as a pivot
between major movements — and is used once. It needs quieter space around it and should not sit
against another high-drama band that competes for the same gravity.

## Contract

Assumes one genuinely manifesto-grade idea worth an entire band, stated in a few resonant words. It
expects restraint around it — the surrounding page must give it room, since its power comes from
standing alone.

## Judgement

Used well, a single defining belief is given the stage and reframes the whole page. The tempting
misuse is dressing a list or an ordinary line as a manifesto, or repeating the device. When the
statement is not truly manifesto-grade, the scale and the space around it only magnify how little
it actually says.
