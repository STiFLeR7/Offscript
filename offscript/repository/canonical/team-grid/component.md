# team-grid

Portrait card grid; genuine portraits required.

## Purpose

The portrait grid: a set of real team members shown together as portraits with names and roles, making a
broad organization legible and human. It exists to convert an abstract promise into the actual people
who own it — breadth of credible faces answering "there are real people behind this," at a glance.

## Choose when

- The people are part of the credibility argument and there are enough of them that breadth is the point.
- A broad team is worth showing — a set of real portraits, each with a genuine name and responsibility.
- The reader is assessing whether real, credible people stand behind the offering.
- Faces and roles, scanned quickly, carry the reassurance without long story copy.

## Avoid when

- The credibility rests on a few executives — reach for a formal leadership row.
- The team is small and a founders story is stronger — reach for the minimal founders row.
- The about page wants people paired with narrative — reach for the team-plus-story hybrid.
- The people are not part of the credibility argument — reach for another proof role.

## Character

Human and credible, presented not interacted with: a grid of genuine portraits scanned for faces and
roles. Its reassurance scales with breadth — many real people, each nameable and accountable — and it
stays calm and factual, letting the specificity of real names and responsibilities do the work.

## Composition

It sits where the people are the argument — typically about or company pages — as a grid of portraits
with names and roles, and supports the ask rather than making it. It should show genuine team members at
a size where names and roles read, never inflate into decorative head-count, and never crowd out the
story a richer page might want.

## Contract

Assumes genuine portraits of actual team members, each with a real name and real responsibility. It
expects every face and role to be real and permitted; invented faces, stock stand-ins, or fabricated
titles betray the trust it trades on, and a wall of decorative head-count with no real specificity reads
as filler.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader assessing the organization sees a broad set of real, credible people who own the
promise, and trusts it more. The tempting misuse is padding the grid with anonymous or stock faces to
look larger, or showing people where the people are not actually the argument. Its credibility is
specificity — real names, real roles — and a single invented face forfeits it.
