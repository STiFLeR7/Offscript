# hero-agent

Conversational/demo hero with intent capture; needs a live-demo story.

## Purpose

The opening screen for a product best understood by trying it, not reading about it. It answers the
engagement problem at the very top of the page: rather than asking the reader to absorb a claim, it
invites them into a short, live exchange that demonstrates the product and captures their intent in
the same motion.

## Choose when

- The product genuinely does something interactive a reader can experience in seconds — a real
  live-demo story exists to invite them into.
- The audience learns by doing and arrives ready to engage, not merely to browse.
- Capturing intent early matters — the first screen doubles as the moment a lead begins.
- A short exchange demonstrates the value more convincingly than any static description could.

## Avoid when

- There is no real, working demo behind the invitation — a hero that promises interaction it cannot
  deliver breaks trust at once; reach for a product-state or calm credibility opening instead.
- The reader is cold and evaluative and wants proof before they will engage — reach for a proof-tile
  opening.
- The page's job at the top is simply to state a promise and step aside — reach for a quieter
  promise-led opening.

## Character

Inviting and live — warmer and more energetic than a static hero. It is the one opening in the family
that is operated as well as read: it offers a real thing to do, with the primary action woven into
the exchange itself rather than sitting beside it.

## Composition

It opens the page and needs room to breathe, so little should crowd it. It hands off to a feature or
how-it-works section that explains what the reader just experienced, and later to proof that the
experience generalises. It should not sit beside another hero, nor be followed immediately by a
second interactive block competing for the same engagement.

## Contract

Assumes a genuine interactive experience exists and can run at the top of the page. It expects a real
live-demo story and a way to hold the intent it provokes — the one promise, the exchange, and the
follow-through; without a working demo behind it the invitation is hollow.

## Judgement

Used well, an interactive product opens by letting the reader try it and quietly capturing their
interest as they do. The tempting misuse is staging an exchange the product cannot honour, or
choosing a demo opening when there is nothing live to demonstrate. When the promised interaction does
not work, the reader's first experience is a broken one and the page never recovers; run without a
real story to capture against, the intent it gathers leads nowhere.
