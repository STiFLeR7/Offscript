# sticky-cards

Long-form storytelling walk; long pages only.

## Purpose

Carries the reader through a long-form story as a guided walk, each idea holding its place while
the next arrives. It solves the lost-in-a-long-page problem: when a narrative needs several beats
to land, a held, walking treatment keeps the thread continuous instead of letting a long scroll
dissolve into disconnected blocks.

## Choose when

- There is a genuine narrative with several beats that benefit from being walked, not skimmed.
- The page is long enough to host an extended, attention-holding passage.
- The story's order matters — each beat sets up the next.
- The audience is engaged and willing to be carried through a longer arc.

## Avoid when

- The page is short — a long walk has nowhere to unfold; reach for a compact feature band instead.
- The content is a quick scan rather than a story — an even grid respects the reader's time.
- More than one long passage is wanted — competing walks exhaust a single page's attention budget.

## Character

A narrative, unfolding, immersive band: ideas revealed in sequence as the reader moves, with a
steady, story-led energy. Density accumulates beat by beat rather than arriving all at once, and
the section is experienced as a passage, not scanned as a list.

## Composition

It lives deep within a long, editorial page, with quieter bands framing it, and hands off into
proof or a close once the story has resolved. It should not appear on a short page, nor twice on
one page, where two walks would blur together.

## Contract

Assumes a long page with room to spare and a story whose beats genuinely build toward a point. It
expects each card to carry a real step of the narrative — not padding stretched to fill the walk.

## Judgement

Used well, a longer story arrives as one continuous, memorable passage. The tempting misuse is
deploying the walk where there is no story, or on a page too short to hold it. When the beats do
not build, the walk feels like scrolling for its own sake and the reader disengages before the
point lands.
