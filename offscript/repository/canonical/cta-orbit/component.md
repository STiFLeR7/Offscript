# cta-orbit

Decorated flagship close; once per page.

## Purpose

The flagship finale: a decorated, high-drama closing built around a creative panel, staged as the
page's crescendo. It solves the memorable-ending problem for a marquee page — when the close should
feel like an arrival rather than a footnote, it gives the single ask a designed, expressive setting so
the last impression carries weight.

## Choose when

- The page is a marquee or campaign moment that deserves a designed, high-energy finale.
- One expressive, decorated close will be more memorable than a plain band — and it is used only once.
- The audience has been fully carried by the argument and is ready for a confident, celebratory ask.
- A creative panel genuinely elevates the close rather than merely dressing it.

## Avoid when

- The page is workaday and a decorated finale would feel overwrought — reach for a plain banner or a
  calm reveal.
- A decorated closing shape is already used on the page — the family allows the heavy close at most
  once.
- A near-footer hand-off with onward links fits better — reach for the close with link columns.
- The moment wants a gentle, low-pressure nudge — reach for a softer mid-page invitation.

## Character

Expressive and high-energy — the most visually charged close in the family, lifted by both a
contrasting surface and a creative figure. It is a crescendo, not a punctuation mark, and it earns its
drama by appearing only once. Its single primary action stays the focal point beneath the decoration.

## Composition

It occupies the page's terminal, flagship position, after everything has been argued, handing off to
the footer. It stands alone: never beside another call-to-action, never repeated, and never crowded by
a second decorated or contrasting band competing for the same crescendo.

## Contract

Assumes a marquee page whose argument justifies a designed finale, and that genuine creative material
exists to carry the panel. It expects one primary action and real creative substance; used more than
once per page, or without true material behind the decoration, the drama turns hollow.

## Judgement

Used well, a flagship page ends on one confident, beautifully-staged ask that readers remember. The
tempting misuse is reaching for spectacle on a page that did not earn it, or deploying the decorated
close more than once so its impact flattens. Overused it becomes noise and every instance loses force;
decorated without substance it reads as style covering for a thin argument.
