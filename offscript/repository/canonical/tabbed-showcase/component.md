# tabbed-showcase

Three-to-five parallel use-cases, one panel at a time.

## Purpose

Presents a handful of parallel use-cases and lets the reader pick the one that matches them,
showing a single panel at a time. It solves the many-audiences problem: when one offering serves
several distinct cases, a switcher lets each reader self-select and see only the story that speaks
to them, instead of wading through all of them.

## Choose when

- There are roughly three to five genuinely parallel use-cases of comparable importance.
- Readers arrive with different needs and will recognise their own among the options.
- Showing every case at once would be long and diffuse — focus beats completeness here.
- The section's job is "this fits your situation", told one situation at a time.

## Avoid when

- One use-case is really the point and the others are minor — reach for a dominance-led band so
  the main case leads.
- The cases must be compared side by side — reach for a comparison or an even grid instead.
- There are only one or two cases, or so many that the switch becomes a menu to hunt through.

## Character

An interactive, focused band: peers presented through a switch, one panel visible at a time, so
attention stays on a single story. Density is medium and contained, the energy is calm and
self-directed, and the reader operates it lightly to find their fit.

## Composition

It belongs mid-page, after the core value is established, where a reader is asking "does this fit
my case?". It hands off to proof or a closing ask, and sits poorly directly beside another
switcher that asks the reader to operate two controls in a row.

## Contract

Assumes three-to-five genuinely parallel, comparable use-cases, each substantial enough to fill a
panel, and a reader who can recognise their own. It expects each case to stand on its own, since
only one is seen at a time.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader taps straight to their situation and reads a story written for them. The
tempting misuse is forcing unequal cases into equal tabs, so the one that matters is buried beside
filler — or offering so many tabs the reader gives up. When a reader's case is missing or hidden,
the switcher quietly tells them the product is not for them.
