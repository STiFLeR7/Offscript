# testimonial-bento

Multi-voice set; shows breadth.

## Purpose

The breadth shape: several real voices shown together so the reader sees not one endorsement but a
chorus. It answers the "is this just one happy customer?" doubt by presenting a set of genuine
testimonies at once, arguing that many kinds of customer — not a lucky exception — have been served
well.

## Choose when

- Breadth is the argument: the strength of the proof is that many different customers vouch.
- The reader is reassured by variety — different roles, industries, or use-cases represented.
- Mid-page, where a set of voices can back a range of claims the page has made.
- No single story dominates; the collective weight of several persuades more than any one.

## Avoid when

- One named customer's focused story is the stronger proof — reach for a single quote-plus-metrics
  card.
- Each voice deserves room and length — reach for a one-at-a-time slider or a deep single story.
- The signal is sheer volume of short praise — reach for a review carousel.
- The proof is a mark — reach for a social-proof role — or a number — reach for a stats / outcomes
  role.

## Character

Confident and plural, moderate in density: a composed set of voices read together, each brief enough
to scan yet real enough to trust. It is read, not operated — the breadth is visible at a glance rather
than revealed through interaction.

## Composition

It sits in the body among the claims it supports, and reads well after the page has made several
promises this set of customers can collectively confirm. It should not sit beside another testimonials
band covering the same voices, nor be so dense that no single quote can be read.

## Contract

Assumes several real, permitted quotes with genuine attribution and, ideally, faces. It expects each
voice in the set to be real and specific; a grid padded with composite or vague endorsements reads as
manufactured consensus and undoes the very breadth it claims.

## Judgement

Used well, a range of named customers together show the product serves many kinds of buyer. The
tempting misuse is filling the set with thin or invented voices to look widely loved, or choosing
breadth when one deep story would persuade more. Any fabricated voice poisons the whole set;
over-packed, it becomes an unreadable wall where no single testimony lands.
