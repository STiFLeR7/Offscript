# reviews-carousel

Many short reviews; the volume-of-love signal.

## Purpose

The volume shape: many short reviews moving past, so sheer quantity of praise becomes the signal. It
answers the "is this widely liked?" question not with one deep story but with the weight of numbers — a
stream of brief, real reviews that together say "very many people are happy," the volume-of-love beat.

## Choose when

- The persuasive signal is quantity: lots of short, genuine reviews outweigh any single one.
- The audience trusts crowd sentiment — a busy stream of positive voices reassures them.
- Late, reinforcing a decision the page has otherwise argued, with breadth of approval.
- Short, scannable praise fits better than long quotes or deep stories.

## Avoid when

- One named customer's specific result is the stronger proof — reach for a single quote-plus-metrics
  card.
- Each voice needs room and length — reach for a one-at-a-time slider or a deep story.
- A curated set showing distinct customer types matters more than raw count — reach for a multi-voice
  set.
- The proof is a mark — reach for a social-proof role — or a number — reach for a stats / outcomes
  role.

## Character

Lively and plural, low in per-item depth but high in cumulative reassurance: a moving stream of short,
real reviews whose collective volume is the message. It is the family's crowd shape — reviews pass one
after another — and the motion signals abundance rather than inviting close study.

## Composition

It sits late in the body, reinforcing claims the page has already made, near the closing ask. It
should not carry the whole proof burden alone, nor sit beside another testimonials band; it
complements deeper proof rather than replacing it.

## Contract

Assumes a genuine volume of real, permitted reviews with at least light attribution. It expects the
reviews to be real and numerous enough that volume is credible; a thin trickle dressed up as a crowd,
or invented reviews, turns the volume signal into a liability.

## Judgement

Used well, a steady stream of real short reviews shows a product that many people genuinely like. The
tempting misuse is manufacturing volume, or reaching for the crowd signal when one specific, named
result would convince a careful buyer more. Faked or sparse, the "everyone loves this" claim rings
false; leaned on alone, it substitutes noise for the specific proof a decision needs.
