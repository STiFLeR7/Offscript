# how-it-works

Canonical three-to-four-step process; rewards longer steps.

## Purpose

The canonical process walk: three-to-four substantial, ordered steps that show, in sequence, how the
offering actually happens from start to result. It exists to convert doubt about feasibility into
belief — to make "I could actually do this" credible by revealing the whole path, each step given enough
room to reward dwelling.

## Choose when

- The content is genuinely a causal sequence — each step follows from the one before.
- There are three to four substantial steps, each worth pausing on.
- The reader understands what the offering is and now needs to believe it is achievable.
- It sits after the "what" and before the proof or the ask, where feasibility is the open question.

## Avoid when

- The items are parallel, not sequential — reach for a feature / value-prop role; order imposed on
  unordered items misleads.
- The process is only a brief lead-in to the ask — reach for the lighter closing-half version.
- There is really one step, or so many that the sequence stops rewarding attention.
- A single step would resolve everything and hand the reader a place to leave.

## Character

Methodical and reassuring: a guided walk read in order, not scanned. Each step opens an expectation the
next step closes, so the reader is carried forward rather than left to browse. It is substantial by
design — the depth of each step is what makes the path feel real — and calm rather than urgent.

## Composition

It sits mid-page, after the offering has been explained and before the proof or the ask, as an ordered
sequence with room for supporting media and an optional next action at its end. Steps must stay in true
causal order, each one legible on its own even to a reader who lands in the middle, and none may quietly
resolve the whole journey early.

## Contract

Assumes a genuine ordered sequence of steps — real, causal, and substantial — with optional supporting
media and a next action. It expects the order to be honest: impose sequence on parallel items and it
deceives; let one step close every loop and the reader has been given permission to leave before the ask.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader who was unsure the offering was achievable finishes the walk believing the path is
real and that they could follow it. The tempting misuse is dressing parallel features as ordered steps,
or padding the sequence past the point where each step earns its dwell. Its whole power is ordered
revelation — break the order, or break the open loops between steps, and it stops demonstrating
feasibility.
