# value-who

"Who we are" plus numbers; bridges value and team.

## Purpose

Bridges the value argument and the people behind it, pairing a short "who we are" with proof so
identity arrives backed by evidence. It solves the disembodied-claim problem: when a reader needs
to know both what the offering delivers and who stands behind it, this band ties the organisation's
identity to real numbers in one move.

## Choose when

- The page needs to establish credibility of the organisation alongside the value it offers.
- A reader's trust depends on knowing both who this is and that the claims are proven.
- The narrative is moving from "what this does" toward "who does it", and proof should travel with
  it.
- There are real numbers that lend weight to the identity being introduced.

## Avoid when

- The page needs a pure team or founder treatment — reach for a team / about band instead.
- The point is value alone with no identity to introduce — reach for a plain value band.
- There is identity to assert but no real proof to attach — claims without evidence read as
  self-congratulation.

## Character

A credibility-building band of medium density that is warm but evidenced: it introduces an
identity and immediately steadies it with numbers. The energy is confident and human rather than
boastful, balancing self-description with proof.

## Composition

It sits mid-page, bridging a value argument into team or about material, with the harder proof
bands behind it and the human story ahead. It should not open the page, and it reads awkwardly
directly beside a pure team band that repeats the same introduction.

## Contract

Assumes a genuine organisational identity worth introducing and real numbers that support it. It
expects the two halves to reinforce each other — the claim about who this is must be backed by the
figures beside it.

## Judgement

Used well, a reader meets the organisation and, in the same breath, sees the proof that its claims
hold. The tempting misuse is a vanity "about us" with no evidence, or burying the value under
self-description. When identity is asserted without proof, the band reads as fluff and weakens the
very credibility it set out to build.
