# Decision Register — Website Track

> **What this is.** The standing record of every governance contradiction that has been resolved, and
> the only admissible route by which a contradiction becomes a governance change.
>
> **Why it exists.** Under the Governance Stability Rule, no governance document may be edited
> directly to solve an implementation problem. A change must originate from one of four triggers: a
> new reference observation, a failed verifier predicate, a broken traceability chain, or a
> contradiction recorded **here**. A row in this file is what makes an amendment legitimate rather
> than speculative — and a reversal made without one is how a rulebook rots.
>
> **How to use it.** Add the row *before* the edit, never after. Every row names the contradiction,
> the rule it breaks, the resolution, and the reversal it creates. Keep the resolution with the affected reference rule so its scope can be audited.

## DR-1 · Multiple accent surfaces in one band

**Contradiction.** `Frame 2147226587` composes a metric row of white, blue **and** purple surfaces
inside a single band.
**Rule it breaks.** One accent per section (`rulebooks/visual-language.md` §15) — now enforced as
harness check 20.
**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29, by the Example Brand 2026 direction).
**Resolution.** The positive rule this row asked for is **accent density**: one *dominant* accent per
section, and at most **three accent moments** in it. That distinguishes the two cases the ceiling
could not. A metric row of white / blue / purple surfaces is a deliberate set — three moments, one
dominant — and is legitimate. Orange → orange → purple → green → blue down a page is not, and was
legal under a budget with no count. The ≤20% budget continues to govern area; the count governs
occurrence, and neither substitutes for the other.
**Reversal created.** Narrows a rule previously stated without exception — "one accent per section"
becomes "one *dominant* accent per section, ≤3 moments".

## DR-2 · Depth on a composited creative

**Contradiction.** `Frame 2147226585` composites an interface panel carrying a light rim and a glow
over an environmental creative.
**Rule it breaks.** "No glass, no backdrop blur, no glow, no depth simulation of any kind"
(`rulebooks/design-charter.md`; `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §12) — enforced as harness check 2.
**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29, by the Example Brand 2026 direction).
**Resolution.** As proposed, and the direction supplies the missing warrant: *"Photography, creative
assets and UI screenshots are provided externally and are not generated by the system. The system
must only determine their placement, sizing, framing and relationship to surrounding content."* A
treatment baked into a delivered asset is therefore outside the system's authority to add **or to
remove**. The no-depth rule continues to govern every surface, card and component the system itself
draws; widening it further would retire the rule.
**Reversal created.** First exception to a rule previously stated as absolute — scoped to delivered
creative, and only because the system does not author it.

## DR-3 · Filled icon chips

**Contradiction.** `Frame 2147226584` and `Frame 2147226586` place filled orange and purple icon
tiles overlapping a creative's edge.
**Rule it breaks.** "No fills except defined chip tiles" (`rulebooks/visual-language.md` §9).
**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29, by the Example Brand 2026 direction).
**Resolution.** The filled icon chip becomes a *defined* chip tile — the rule already anticipates the
category and simply has nothing in it. The direction supplies both halves: it sanctions the form
(orange serves "floating icons" and "corner accents") while holding the outline rule for the mark
itself ("avoid filled icons"). Those read as contradictory only until the chip and the glyph are
separated: **the tile is a filled surface; the glyph inside it stays outline.** Owned by the
Iconography discipline under Occasion, with a rule for when a chip appears rather than a licence to
fill.
**Reversal created.** None. This fills a category the rule already reserves.

## DR-4 · A human subject as the creative

**Contradiction.** `Frame 2147226586` uses a human portrait as the section's creative.
**Rule it breaks.** Environmental imagery has one source, and this asset is not among the seven
(`rulebooks/creatives.md`).
**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29, by the Example Brand 2026 direction).
**Resolution.** Covered by the second approved asset class, worded broadly as the row required —
the direction's own wording is *"photography, creative assets and UI screenshots are provided
externally"*, which reaches editorial and human photography without being scoped to interface
creative. The asset-kind vocabulary now carries `People` explicitly, with a governed placement
(narrative) and a crop constraint (never crop faces).
**Reversal created.** None. Extends a library; the select-never-generate rule is untouched.

## DR-5 · The reserved discipline slot is named for something other than what it governs

**Contradiction.** The Foundation charter reserves a discipline named **Experience Dynamics**
(`FOUNDATION.md`, `FOUNDATION_MODEL_TEMPLATE.md`). The discipline being authored into that slot
governs *how a section should feel before it is built* — art direction. "Experience Dynamics" names
neither that subject nor anything else the stack defines, and reads as motion or interaction.
**Rule it engages.** The naming discipline — name what is governed.
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** Author the discipline as **`EDITORIAL_ART_DIRECTION.md`**, named for its subject, and
record in its header that it is the discipline the charter reserves as Experience Dynamics. The
charter is **not edited**: its list ends "and any future discipline", so it is an open enumeration
rather than a closed registry, and no amendment is required to author into it. This also keeps
`FOUNDATION.md` — one of the twelve constitution-tier documents `DEV-HANDOFF.md` records as
deliberately untouched — untouched.
**Reversal created.** None. Nothing is renamed; a reserved slot is filled under an accurate name.

## DR-6 · Felt register is bound per archetype, but must vary between adjacent sections

**Contradiction.** Two requirements pulled against each other. The felt register was to be added to
the §A2 behavioural contract (bound per archetype), *and* adjacent sections were to be required to
differ in register. Both cannot hold: two adjacent sections legitimately sharing an archetype would
necessarily share a register, and the adjacency rule could never fail — **a check that cannot fail**,
which is the precise defect Phase 1 was spent finding.
**Rule it engages.** `COMPOSE.md` §A2.1 — every field is a predicate or explicitly marked rationale;
a declaration that constrains nothing is not a decision.
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** The contract binds a **permitted band** of registers per archetype; the **section
declares one value from within that band**. This is the shape the system already uses for creative
presence, where `media-first` permits `l`–`xl` and each section takes one. The archetype constrains;
the instance decides. Verified against the live page before authoring: sections 9 and 10 are adjacent
and both `editorial-stack`, so `editorial-stack` must permit at least two registers for the adjacency
rule to be falsifiable at all.
**Reversal created.** None — this corrects a contradiction inside the implementation plan before it
reached a document.

# 2026-07-29 — Example Brand 2026 Creative Direction

> Seven rows admitted together, all originating from the same trigger: **a new reference
> observation** — a creative-direction brief from the Creative Director. Four of them are genuine
> reversals of rules authored the previous day, and they are recorded here *before* the edits rather
> than explained after them. The remaining three close contradictions the register already carried.

## DR-7 · A photograph carrying text

**Contradiction.** The direction states that photography is never used as a background behind text,
in the hero or in any section — *"avoid using image in hero section as background or for any
section."* Governance mandates the opposite: `immersive-hero` is specified as an environment the
type *inhabits*, and an entire measured veil system exists to make that legible.
**Rule it breaks.** `rulebooks/creatives.md` (hero — typography occupies the negative space);
`component-governance/COMPOSE.md` §A2.2 (Immersive Hero relationship + forbidden states);
`rulebooks/visual-language.md` §1.1 (the measured veil); `foundation/ILLUSTRATION.md`;
`OVERVIEW.md`.
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** A photograph is a **subject**, never a surface. No composed surface carries both a
photograph and text. The veil ramp, the scrim tokens and the photographic-surface base are
**deprecated in place and inert** — the treatment elevation and glass already received — so nothing
downstream breaks and referencing one is a violation rather than a fallback. The `immersive-hero`
archetype is **redefined, not deleted**: a full-bleed creative that is the section's subject and
carries no type. Deleting it would collapse the eight-signature distinctness matrix and orphan two
registers it alone permits. Film and grain **on a creative shown as subject** survive untouched —
that is material on an asset, not a surface. Text on a **solid** accent band is unaffected.
**Reversal created.** Yes, and a large one. It retires the veil system authored 2026-07-28,
including the measurement that justified it (1.02:1 unveiled → 7.37:1 veiled). The measurement was
correct; the direction removes the case it measured. Two catalog entries in `brand-pack/ASSETS.md`
become dead letters and are re-catalogued as subjects — `env-lake-mirror` ("a closing *surface*")
and `env-massif-clear` ("too detailed to sit behind copy").

## DR-8 · Iconography — scarcity against a design language

**Contradiction.** The direction makes icons a core part of the visual grammar, present throughout
the page, and instructs the system to *actively look for opportunities to replace repetitive text
patterns with semantic iconography* — twenty-six named occasions. The discipline protects the
opposite property.
**Rule it breaks.** `foundation/ICONOGRAPHY.md` §8 — *"Scarcity is what makes a mark findable… an
interface where most elements carry one has made the mark the baseline, and a mark that is everywhere
cannot direct attention anywhere."* And §9 — *"a mark with no role does not appear."*
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** The two are reconciled by the primitive the discipline already ranks first:
**Occasion**. The direction does not ask for marks without roles — it *enumerates the roles*, which
is precisely what the discipline's own Artifact B records as missing: *"the only governed behaviour
was hygiene — one set, outline, one stroke weight — and **no rule ever caused a mark to appear**."*
So §9's rule is kept verbatim and the occasion catalogue is authored beneath it; what changes is that
the catalogue is now large enough that the default flips from absent to present-where-meaningful.
**The decoration bans are untouched and become the load-bearing constraint** — a mark that repeats
its label is still decoration, and every mark must still communicate meaning.
**Reversal created.** Yes. §8's scarcity paragraph is narrowed: scarcity is no longer a property of
the page but of *each occasion* — one mark per role, not few marks per page. Recorded because a
reader who ports only the occasion catalogue and leaves §8 standing will port a contradiction.

## DR-9 · Equal N-up card grids

**Contradiction.** The direction states that cards never appear as perfectly identical repeated
grids unless comparison is the narrative purpose, and prefers Large / Small / Accent / Neutral over
1 · 1 · 1 · 1. Governance states the equal grid as the rule.
**Rule it breaks.** `rulebooks/visual-language.md` — *"Card stacks lay out as equal N-up rows."*
Supported by the `n-up-card-grid` layout primitive in `COMPOSITION.md`, tagged on ~25 catalog rows.
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** The equal grid is narrowed from the default to the **`feature-comparison`** case,
where parallel structure *is* the message. Everywhere else cards carry differentiated behavioural
intent. This is less a reversal than a correction: `COMPOSE.md` §C.14's 1.5× focal margin already
fails a three-equal-card grid unless the section declares `distributed`, so the rule and the grammar
were already in tension and the grammar was winning silently.
**Reversal created.** Yes — a rule stated without exception becomes a rule scoped to one archetype.

## DR-10 · Symmetry as a legal choice

**Contradiction.** The direction refuses mechanical symmetry: no perfectly mirrored layouts, no
mathematically equal visual weight. Governance offers symmetry as an equally legitimate answer.
**Rule it breaks.** `SECTION_LAYOUT.md` §13 — *"A unit **may** be composed symmetrically — weight
answered by equal weight — or asymmetrically."*
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** Symmetry narrows from a free choice to a **declared exception with a stated reason**.
The sentence that survives is the one that already carried the argument — *"a progression composed
symmetrically throughout has not chosen symmetry; it has declined to compose"* — now applied per
unit rather than per progression. **One exception is named rather than left implicit:** the closing
band remains centred, because `COMPOSE.md` §C.12 argues centring there as the shape of convergence,
and the CTA placement rule now carries that as a positive rule rather than an exception.
**Reversal created.** Yes. A permission becomes a justification requirement.

## DR-11 · Two archetype vocabularies

**Contradiction.** The direction names twelve composition archetypes. `COMPOSE.md` §A2 holds a
**closed set of eight**, each a behavioural contract over five signature dimensions, under a
regression rule that forbids two contracts from matching.
**Rule it engages.** `COMPOSE.md` §A2.1 (predicate-or-rationale) and §A2.3 (*if uniqueness
decreases, the change does not ship*).
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** The twelve names are mapped onto the contract system rather than replacing it, because
a name without a distinct signature constrains nothing. Three already exist. `Immersive Landscape`
**renames** `immersive-hero` under DR-7. `Narrative Left`/`Right` are **anchor values** of
`editorial-split`, which already binds both. `Process Layout` and `Timeline Composition` are two names
for `narrative-flow`. `Feature Stack` and `Full-width Editorial` are `editorial-stack`, the latter
widening containment — a contract edit that re-runs the matrix. `Proof Layout` and `Storytelling
Composition` require **new contract rows**, and ship only if each can be given a signature distinct
from every other. If one cannot, it does not ship as an archetype.
**Reversal created.** None. The set widens; the regression rule that governs it is untouched and is
what constrains the widening.

## DR-12 · Register vocabulary replacement

**Contradiction.** The direction names ten section personalities. `rulebooks/editorial-art-direction.md`
holds a closed eight-register vocabulary authored 2026-07-28.
**Rule it engages.** The vocabulary is closed by design; replacing it invalidates the permitted-band
table that binds registers to archetypes.
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** **Replace the vocabulary, keep the mechanism.** The ten names become the register set;
the permitted-band table is re-authored against them. Predicate **P3** (no two adjacent sections
declare the same register) and the archetype-constrains / instance-decides shape resolved in
**DR-6** carry over unchanged — DR-6's finding still binds: a band must permit at least two registers
per archetype or the adjacency rule cannot fail.
**Reversal created.** The vocabulary only. No mechanism, predicate or contract shape changes.

## DR-13 · Section declaration widens from one field to six

**Contradiction.** The direction requires a section to declare narrative importance, why it exists,
what makes it memorable and how concentrated it is — none of which the contract carries. Today a
section declares an archetype and a register, and two sections identical on both can still be
different pieces of communication.
**Rule it engages.** `COMPOSE.md` §A2.1 — a contract field is a predicate or is marked rationale.
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** The contract widens to six ordered fields: **Hierarchy → Intent → Archetype →
Register → Signature → Density**. Each is a predicate. Ordering matters and is one-way: Intent keys
the component grammar, and Hierarchy governs the scale, space and emphasis the rest inherit. The
existing §A2.3 regression rule extends to the widened contract.
**Reversal created.** None. The contract gains fields; nothing it already asserted changes.

## DR-14 · Two measures, and only two — titles included

**Contradiction.** The direction states that **body and title width are never anything except 950px
or 750px**. Three things in the system contradict it at once: the reading band had just been set to
760/920 earlier the same day; the **editorial measure** that h1–h3 take was 1100px, a third value
that applies specifically to titles; and the closing band's pair had been collapsed onto the reading
band, so it moved with it.
**Rule it breaks.** `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §5 — the four-width model, whose Editorial
measure is a distinct third text width. And the pack's own 760/920 values, authored hours earlier
under the same directive's *"reading content 760–920px"*.
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** **Two text measures exist: 750 and 950. Nothing else.** Titles take the wide value,
body takes the wide or the narrow one, and the closing band's pair is the same two values reached
for differently. The `--cr-measure-editorial` token is **re-pointed to the wide measure rather than
deleted**, because ~a dozen call sites consume it by name and the name still describes a real role —
what changes is that the role no longer has a width of its own.

**The four-width model survives this, and that is worth stating** because it looks like it should
not. Its argument was never *three text widths*; it was that **the narrowing between two widths is
what carries hierarchy**. A title at 950 above body at 750 is exactly that narrowing, performed with
the two values the direction permits. What is retired is the third value, not the mechanism — and
the container width (1440) is untouched, since it is not a text measure.

*A note on the earlier value.* 760/920 came from reading *"reading content 760–920px"* as the
authoritative pair. That was a defensible reading and it was wrong: the numbers were describing a
**band** the two real measures sit inside, not replacing them. The corrected rule is narrower than
either reading — not a range at all, but a closed pair.

**Reversal created.** Yes, twice over. It reverses the 760/920 values set earlier today, and it
retires the editorial measure as an independent width — a rule that had stood since the four-width
model was authored.

## DR-15 · The title measure is 750, and 950 is a release valve

**Contradiction.** The direction states that **titles mostly take 750px, and reach 950px only when
the title runs to a third line.** DR-14 had just set the title measure to a flat 950. Two things now
break: the title measure is no longer a single value with a role, and — more consequentially — the
rule directly above it in `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §5 says *headlines and supporting copy
INTENTIONALLY occupy different measures, and the transition between them establishes the section's
visual hierarchy.* With title and body both at 750 by default, **that transition does not happen.**
**Rule it breaks.** `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §5 (the title→body narrowing as a hierarchy
mechanism); `COMPOSE.md` §C.4; the closing band's standing status as the one place a measure pair
exists (`colors_and_type.css`, `components.md`).
**Status.** **Resolved.**
**Resolution.** **750 is the title measure. 950 is a release valve, reached for because the type
wrapped to a third line and for no other reason.** This is not a new mechanism — it is the closing
band's rule, generalized. The band had `--cr-measure-cta-narrow` / `-wide` with exactly this
semantics, documented in the pack as *"a RELEASE VALVE for a headline that runs to a third line."*
Every title now works the way the closing band already did, and **the closing band stops being an
exception at all** — its pair is the general pair.

**The narrowing is retired as a hierarchy mechanism, and this is the substantive part.** §5 made the
title→body width transition load-bearing for hierarchy. It cannot be, once both default to the same
value. **Hierarchy comes from scale, weight and space** — 72px against 20px, and the rhythm roles
between them — which is what `rulebooks/visual-language.md`'s own governing posture has said all
along: *visual hierarchy is created with typography, scale, whitespace, alignment and proportion.*
Measure was one mechanism among those, it was the weakest of them, and it is the one that produced a
third width twice in two days. Removing it costs nothing the others were not already carrying.

*Why a tight default is right rather than merely permitted:* a 950px line at the body size is ~110
characters, well past comfortable measure, and the approved reference frames show headlines wrapping
inside a narrow column rather than running wide. The wide value exists for the case where a
three-line headline reads as a stack rather than a statement — a wrap problem, not a hierarchy one.

**The realization is an authored opt-in, and deliberately so.** CSS cannot count rendered lines, so
the wide value is reached through a class, exactly as the closing band's already is. That is not a
gap: **the harness can count lines even though CSS cannot**, so "the valve was pulled without cause"
becomes a checkable condition rather than an honour system — a title capped at the wide value while
rendering in two lines fails.
**Reversal created.** Two. The title measure moves from a flat 950 (DR-14, the same day) to a
750-with-release-valve. And the title→body narrowing stops being a hierarchy mechanism, which is a
rule that predates both.

## DR-16 · A section's selectable identity is one cell of one column

**Contradiction.** The direction requires the library to be selected by structural appropriateness —
*"which existing structure most effectively communicates the intended message"*. The catalog's
`Serves` column is documented as **"generous by layout — it lists intents the structure can carry,
not just the component's current copy"**, but selection compares the requirement against the **first
listed value only**. Everything after the first comma is unreadable, so the generosity the column
claims does not exist where it matters.

**Rule it breaks.** `COMPOSITION.md`'s own selection posture — *"pick by intent-fit … a component's
category is its **origin**, not its only use"* — which the shipped gate cannot honour, and the
Playbook's cross-category picks, which exist only as prose nothing can read.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29, by the Section Library Intelligence Pass).

**Resolution.** Measured against the catalog itself, not inferred: of 65 selectable sections, the
requirement `feature` is listed by 31 and winnable by **7**; `cta` 19 → 7; `stats` 13 → 4; `hero`
9 → 4; `integrations` 7 → 2; **`comparison` 3 → 1**, so one section answers that requirement always.
The cause is not accumulated preference — **nothing in the library accumulates**, and the per-section
use-count store is absent, so cross-page rotation is constant-zero. The cause is arity: a structure
that can carry eight kinds of information is permitted one word about itself.

Every section therefore declares a **capability profile** — twenty fields plus two marked rationale,
each binding a vocabulary that already has an owner — and intent becomes an **unordered set**.
Fitness is then a comparison between the page's declaration and the library's, computed at
composition time from (requirement × profile) and **never stored**. There is no per-section prior, so
there is nothing to accumulate into; equal opportunity holds by construction rather than by
rebalancing.

**Reversal created.** Position in the `Serves` list stops being meaningful. Until the selector is
replaced, row order remains load-bearing — the catalog and the gate must land together, or primary
intent is silently undefined.

## DR-17 · Contextual confidence replaces rotation among equals

**Contradiction.** The direction states that **confidence must be contextual rather than historical**
and that *"the goal is not to maximise variation — the goal is to maximise appropriateness"*. The
catalog instructs the opposite: *"among equals, rotate — prefer the one used **least recently**"*,
and the standing engineering remedy would promote least-recently-used into the primary variety force.
Recency is history, and history is what the direction removes.

**Rule it breaks.** `COMPOSITION.md`'s "Pick by intent-fit, then rotate among equals
(anti-default-collapse)".

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29).

**Resolution.** Fitness decides. Genuine ties break off **the page's own declaration** — register not
adjacent-equal, signature not repeated, density recovery satisfied, archetype not repeated — all of
which are contextual, deterministic, and already governed. Least-recently-used across pages survives
only as a last resort reached when the page itself cannot discriminate; catalog order is below it.

*Why rotation is demoted rather than deleted:* it was doing real work only because the profile was
too thin for anything else to. With twenty axes, the page-context tie-breaks resolve almost every
tie on communication grounds, and a tie that survives them is one where freshness costs nothing.
**Variety becomes a consequence of honest profiles and a diagnostic of dishonest ones — never an
objective the selector optimises.**

**Reversal created.** Two. Recency moves from the anti-collapse mechanism to a last resort. And
"maximise variety", stated in the first direction round, yields to "maximise appropriateness" stated
in the second; the later instruction governs.

## DR-18 · Three new declaration axes — capacity, pace and load

**Contradiction.** The direction requires the Creative Director to reason about pacing —
*"I've had three dense sections, I need breathing room"* — and about quantity fit. `SECTION_LAYOUT.md`
§10 already **requires** density to rest and peak, and the page tier already requires recovery. Those
rules exist, and they are checked over finished output, because the library declares nothing that
would let pacing be **planned** rather than audited.

**Rule it engages.** `COMPOSE.md` §A2.1 — a field is a predicate or is marked rationale; and §A2.3 —
a distinction that does not distinguish does not ship.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29), with an independence condition attached.

**Resolution.** Three axes are added to the library's declaration. **They do not share one owner, and
the search for one is what found the third.** Placing all three beside density in `SECTION_LAYOUT.md`
was the obvious move and would have breached that document's own §16, which states it does **not**
decide communication strategy. Cognitive load is not a spatial property — and it is not new either:
`COMMUNICATION_AND_PERCEPTION_SYSTEM.md` §11 already governs rhythm *as cognitive-energy management*
— *"comprehension consumes cognitive energy, and cognitive energy is finite within a single reading"*
— it simply has no per-unit measure of the energy it rations. The collision named the owner:

| Axis | Owner | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| **Communication capacity** | `SECTION_LAYOUT.md` §10 | how many parts share a space is spatial allocation |
| **Visual pace** | `SECTION_LAYOUT.md` §11 | the speed of movement through a field, beside the shape of it |
| **Cognitive load** | `COMMUNICATION_AND_PERCEPTION_SYSTEM.md` §11 | the per-unit measure of the energy that chapter already rations |

The three axes themselves:

- **Communication capacity** — `singular · paired · triad · small-set · large-set · continuous`.
  *(Two corrections to the drafted set, made before it shipped: the proposed value `editorial`
  collided head-on with the register of the same name, and a vocabulary cannot carry one word for two
  decisions; and `small-set` at four-to-six against `large-set` at eight-or-more left seven with
  nowhere to go. `paired` is added because two-up structures are real and were unnameable.)*
  *Not density.* Density is how concentrated the unit is *meant* to be; capacity is what quantity the
  structure was *built for*. A three-column layout and a continuous editorial column can both be
  `medium` and cannot hold the same content. Also not the §C.18 ceiling: **capacity is the want, the
  four-group ceiling is the law**, and the law applies regardless of the want.
- **Visual pace** — `fast · moderate · slow`. How many beats the eye takes through the unit.
- **Cognitive load** — `light · medium · heavy`. What comprehension costs.

**The independence condition, and it is a real gate.** Load is claimed distinct from density because
a four-item comparison matrix and a four-item card row carry equal density and unequal load — one
asks the reader to weigh, the other to scan. Pace is claimed distinct from reading flow because a
single oversized statement and a single dense diagram are both `linear` and read at opposite speeds.
Those are arguments, not guarantees. **If either axis proves to be perfectly predicted by density
across all 79 profiles, it is density written twice and it does not ship.** This is §A2.3's
discipline applied to a new vocabulary *before* it becomes load-bearing rather than after.

**Reversal created.** None. Three axes are added; nothing already asserted changes. The density
rhythm and recovery rules are unchanged — they gain an input.

## DR-19 · Audience is declared, not inferred from prose

**Contradiction.** The direction requires each section to declare executive, enterprise, technical
and marketing suitability. The system already has an audience dimension, and it **derives** it by
scanning each catalog row's prose against a closed tag set. That prose was written as human selection
guidance; it was never written to carry an audience claim, so the derivation reads intent into
wording that does not hold it.

**Rule it engages.** `COMPOSE.md` §A2.1 — the distinction between a declared predicate and an
inferred one. A field that can be wrong without anything noticing is not a declaration.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29).

**Resolution.** Audience becomes a declared field on every profile, over the existing closed set —
`executive · enterprise · technical · operational · marketing · specialist` — owned by
`BRAND_EXPRESSION.md` §10.1. The inferred layer is retired behind the declaration rather than removed:
the vocabulary is unchanged, so what changes is where the value comes from, not what it may be.

**And the owner's existing term had to be separated from the new one, not reused.** `BRAND_EXPRESSION.md`
§3 already receives **Audience Context** — who this *website* is for, an organizational truth. What a
structure declares is not that: it is **address**, the reader postures the structure can speak well to.
One website has one Audience Context; one structure can address several. Folding the second into the
first would have made every section appear to name the site's audience, which no section is entitled to
do. The field names the direction asked for — executive, enterprise, technical, marketing suitability —
are kept; what they declare is capability, and the page's Audience Context selects among it.

**Reversal created.** A derived value becomes an authored one. Any drift between the two is now
visible as a diff rather than resolved silently in favour of whichever ran last.

## DR-20 · Two requirements the demand vocabulary never emits

**Contradiction.** The catalog tags `social-proof` and `outcomes` as intents a structure can serve —
14 and 13 sections list them — but neither is ever emitted as a requirement. Two sections whose
**primary** serve is one of them are therefore unreachable as a best-fit pick, permanently, and 27
capability listings point at demand that cannot arise.

**Rule it breaks.** The catalog's guarantee that its `Serves` tags describe selectable capability.
The router's own rule table carries the identical gap, which is what identifies this as drift rather
than a decision.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29).

**Resolution.** With intent an unordered set (DR-16), "primary serve" stops gating reachability and
the two sections cease to be dead. The vocabulary gap itself is still real and is reconciled: both
values are legitimate communication intents and are mapped onto the declared Intent vocabulary rather
than left as orphan tags. **Reachability becomes a standing report** — every section must be a viable
answer to at least one requirement, and any that is not is named, so this class of drift surfaces as
a finding instead of being discovered by tracing the selector.

**Reversal created.** None. Two sections and 27 listings become reachable; nothing that was
selectable stops being so.

## DR-21 · Two fields both claim to select the slot skeleton

**Contradiction.** `COMPOSE.md` §B states *"the skeleton is chosen **BY the section's Composition
Archetype** (§A2), never picked directly."* §A2.4 states *"**Intent** keys the slot sequence in §B."*
Two of the six declared fields claim the same downstream decision, in the same document. Worse, §B's
thirteen skeletons are named by page label — Hero, Feature, Stats, Pricing — so **neither** claim can
be honoured as written: the ten Intent values do not appear there, and neither do the ten archetypes.
Declaring `educate` yields no skeleton, and no mapping exists in either direction.

**Rule it breaks.** `COMPOSE.md` §A2.1 — *"a declared field must constrain what follows it. A field
that any value could satisfy is decoration, not a decision."* Two fields constraining the same thing
means at least one of them is not constraining it.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29, by the Section Library Intelligence Pass).

**Resolution.** Both claims are true once they stop meaning the same thing, and the declaration order
already says which is which — Intent is decided **before** Archetype:

> **Intent determines which slots must be present. Archetype determines how they are arranged.**

An intent to `prove` requires an evidence slot; an intent to `convert` requires an action slot — that
is a statement about *what the section must contain*, and it is settled before anything is placed.
Whether the resulting slots are held side by side, stacked, sequenced or bled is the archetype's
decision, and it is settled after. Neither field can answer the other's question.

The thirteen named skeletons are therefore **demoted from selector to realization**: they are common
(intent × archetype) pairings, recorded because they are useful, not because they are chosen. A
skeleton is never picked by name — the two rules above produce it. This is the same correction §A2
already made one level up, where a geometry tag was found making a communication decision because
nothing sat above it; here a page-label skeleton was making two.

**Reversal created.** The skeleton list stops being a menu. §B's "chosen BY the Composition
Archetype" narrows to *arranged by* it, and gains the slot-requirement half it never had.

## DR-22 · The section library is frozen and must be modernized

**Contradiction.** `DEV-HANDOFF.md` states *"all 79 files remain frozen."* `brand-pack/exemplars/README.md`
states they are *"now pinned **so they cannot half-migrate**,"* and names the failure mode exactly: the
live pack's token names are deliberately stable, so while these files linked it they were *"silently
picking up the new page surface, accents, stroke and radii **underneath** their hardcoded old font-family
strings and raw old hexes. The result was a hybrid that was neither language."* A pilot has now been
commissioned to modernize ten of the seventy-nine to the current direction. Modernizing ten in place is
that same hybrid one level up — a library holding two languages at once — so the freeze and the pilot
cannot both be honoured by editing the files.

**Rule it breaks.** `brand-pack/exemplars/README.md` — the pin, and *"Do not author against
`_frozen-v2-tokens.css`, and do not sync it forward."* Also the amendment standard the handoff sets for
this exact class of change: byte-identity may be broken, but *"it needs a deliberate amendment rather
than a silent break."*

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-29, by the Creative Direction Migration pilot).

**Resolution.** The freeze protects an **artifact**; the pilot asks for a **section under the current
direction**. Both hold as long as the migration *produces* rather than *mutates*:

> **A migration authors a new specimen. It never re-themes a frozen one.**

The pilot therefore runs in four stages, and this row authorizes exactly one of them:

| | | authorized here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frozen v2 — unchanged, permanently | — |
| 2 | v3 specimen, structure preserved | **yes** |
| 3 | Creative review | — |
| 4 | Approved production port | **no** |

Stage 4 has two legs — a port over the frozen originals, and the dev team's port into the runtime copy
under `offscript/`. Neither is authorized by this row; each requires its own, after review. Until then the
frozen library remains the only section library, and every statement pinning it stays true.

**The Pilot Constraint, standing.** A visual modernization pass is **presentation-only; no behavioural
contract may change.** Frozen: DOM hierarchy and order, element types, `id`, `data-*`, `aria-*`, `href`,
`src`, all JavaScript, any class a script references, event listeners, keyboard behaviour, state and
animation logic. Permitted: everything in the presentation layer, plus decorative and presentational
wrappers. Interaction-contract and accessibility modernization is a **separate** Component Contract
Migration, run after the visual language is approved — because a pass that changes both cannot tell which
one broke.

Two readings are settled with it. A permitted **wrapper** is a single-child pass-through or a non-content
decorative sibling: strip the additions and the tree must be identical, which keeps "hierarchy frozen"
and "wrappers allowed" from contradicting each other. And a `data-*` **declaration** is permitted where
the verifier requires one to run a design check, on the condition that **no script reads it** —
declarative metadata is not a behaviour hook, and a check that cannot run is recorded as not-run rather
than silently passed.

**Scope of that second reading, settled by measurement.** Run against a section, the verifier reports
five further checks unowned — *"section 0 declares no hierarchy… no intent… no register… no signature…
no density — the contract is six fields and this one is unowned."* Those are not five separate
attributes; they are `COMPOSE.md` §A2.1's **single six-field declaration**, and the values for all
seventy-nine are already authored and approved in `COMPOSITION.md`. Permitting one field and withholding
the other five would leave the composition checks — the ones that judge whether a section *communicates*,
as against whether it *complies* — unrun on every specimen, which is most of what a creative review is
for. The exception is therefore the **declaration block entire**, under the same single condition: no
script may read any of it, and the migration verifier fails the specimen if one does.

**Reversal created.** None to the freeze: it is unchanged and every statement pinning the library remains
true. What is created is a **second generation existing alongside the first**, and the rule that follows
from it — the two are never mixed within one page, and a section is migrated wholly or not at all. The
half-migration the pin was built to prevent is now prevented by construction rather than by prohibition.

## DR-23 · The palette's deep steps are derived, and the authored values disagree with them

**Contradiction.** The pack holds eight deep accent steps produced by a mechanism, not a choice —
`colors_and_type.css:84`: *"Each is its accent at a fixed OKLCH lightness descent (800 = L−0.22,
900 = L−0.32), hue and chroma held… **mechanism rather than a choice**."* The canonical foundation
authors all eleven steps of each ramp explicitly. Every one of the eight derived values differs from
its authored counterpart, so the derivation and the authored ramp cannot both stand.

**Rule it breaks.** The derivation itself (`colors_and_type.css:84–91`), and `MANIFEST.md`'s
*"single source of VALUES"* — a value cannot have two sources.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-30, by the Canonical Colour Foundation).

**Resolution.** The authored values replace the derived ones. A derivation is a rule for *inventing* a
value, and the foundation's governing constraint is that values are never invented — *never modified,
interpolated, approximated, replaced, or regenerated*. The mechanism is therefore retired rather than
re-tuned, and `colors_and_type.css:84`'s comment goes with it.

| Token | Was (derived) | Now (authored) |
|---|---|---|
| orange-800 / -900 | `#B23000` / `#8F0000` | `#913B00` / `#612600` |
| blue-800 / -900 | `#0072B5` / `#005395` | `#085A81` / `#043851` |
| purple-800 / -900 | `#3F00B1` / `#2F008E` | `#4210A3` / `#2C0A6B` |
| green-800 / -900 | `#003800` / `#001D00` | `#174A25` / `#103319` |

The four base accents are **unchanged** — `#FF7926`, `#26763D`, `#26B7FF`, `#6E26FF` were already
exactly the canonical values. The eleven-step ramps are new; the identity is not.

**Reversal created.** Retires a generative rule. Deep steps are no longer computed from a lightness
descent; they are read from an authored ramp, and adding a hue no longer implies deriving its steps.

## DR-24 · The supplied focus ring cannot be seen

**Contradiction.** The foundation's INTERACTION block binds `--cr-focus-ring` and `--cr-info` to
`blue-500`. Measured on the page cream, `#26B7FF` is **2.26:1** — below the **3:1** WCAG 1.4.11
minimum for a non-text indicator, and far below the **4.5:1** a link needs as text. The shipped ring
is `#2A2A2A` at **14.35:1**. Binding the alias as written would replace a compliant indicator with an
invisible one.

**Rule it breaks.** WCAG 1.4.11 (non-text contrast), and `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §15 — *"Only
accents that meet contrast as text may be used as text."*

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-30, by the Canonical Colour Foundation).

**Resolution.** No hex changes; the **alias** re-points, which the immutability rule does not
constrain. And the resolution is deliberately *not* the obvious one: re-pointing the ring to
`blue-700` (**4.62:1**) would be compliant but would still be a downgrade from 14.35:1, and the
shipped ring was never broken.

| Binding | Resolution | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| default focus ring | **stays dark** `#2A2A2A` | 14.35:1 |
| focus ring on an accent or dark surface | `blue-700` | 4.62:1 |
| `--cr-info` | `blue-700` | 4.62:1 |
| `blue-500` | surface and fill only — **never text, never an indicator** | 2.26:1 as text |

`--cr-primary` needs no amendment: `--cr-on-accent-light` already puts **dark** text on orange at
**5.48:1**. The pack was already correct here, and the spec's implied white label (2.62:1) is what
would have broken it.

**Reversal created.** None to the palette. It narrows two supplied aliases, and records the principle
that **an accessibility floor is not satisfied by moving from failing to merely adequate** when a
compliant value is already shipping.

## DR-25 · Orange is reserved for the primary CTA, and forbidden as a button fill

**Contradiction.** The foundation reserves orange for *"Primary CTA · High emphasis · Primary actions
· Important highlights."* `COMPOSE.md:561` forbids orange as *"a large surface, a card fill, a **button
fill** or a page background."* A primary CTA is ordinarily a filled button, so the two cannot both
stand as written.

**Rule it breaks.** `COMPOSE.md:561`; `colors_and_type.css:60` (*"NEVER a large coloured surface"*).

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-30, by the Canonical Colour Foundation).

**Resolution.** The contradiction dissolves once *"reserved for"* is read as a restriction on **where
orange may appear**, not a requirement that CTAs be orange — and the references confirm that reading.
`Frame 2147226584` uses a **dark pill** for its primary action (near-black covers 10.3% of the frame)
while all warm hues together total **0.886%**; the closing band is **blue** and contains no orange at
all.

The governing principle, which is broader than colour:

> **Colour communicates emphasis. Components communicate function. Layout communicates hierarchy.
> Typography communicates importance.** Colour reinforces hierarchy; it never creates it.

So orange is an **emphasis** colour, never a component identity. The default primary button remains
the high-contrast dark pill. An orange-filled button is a bounded optional variant: **at most one per
page, and only where it is that page's highest-priority action.** Without that bound the variant
becomes the default by drift, which is the failure this row exists to prevent — a permission is not a
rule until it states its limit.

**The reversal is narrower than it looks, and the sweep matters.** Orange is restricted in four
places and only one clause moves:

| Location | Wording | Fate |
|---|---|---|
| `COMPOSE.md:561` | *"never a large surface, a card fill, a **button fill** or a page background"* | **only `button fill` narrows**; large surface, card fill and page background all stand |
| `colors_and_type.css:846` | *"No orange **band**: the accent is a spark, never a wash"* | **survives intact** — a button is not a band |
| `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §15 | *"never used as a large coloured surface"* | **survives** — a button is not a large surface |
| `colors_and_type.css:102` | *"orange 2.38:1 ✗ never text"* | **survives** — about text, not fill |

The orange *band* stays forbidden. Changing one clause while leaving the other three is what keeps the
pack from contradicting its own comments.

**Reversal created.** Narrows one clause of a rule previously stated without exception, and replaces a
prohibition with a bounded permission. The bound is the reversal's price and is not optional.

## DR-26 · The surface numbering implies white is the page, the references say cream

**Contradiction.** The foundation lists `surface-1` (`#FFFFFF`) first, which reads as the base
surface. Every approved frame contradicts it: `#F6F5EF` is the dominant field in all six — **54.7% ·
23.0% · 65.5% · 36.0% · 63.3% · 79.8%**, at exact zero distance — while white appears *on top of* it
as a raised card surface (10.5% and 13.4% in `Frame 2147226587` and `Frame 2147226588`).

**Rule it engages.** Nothing yet stated a default page ground; `--cr-bg` has been `#F6F5EF` in
practice without a rule requiring it.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-30, by the Canonical Colour Foundation).

**Resolution.** `#F6F5EF` is the **canonical default page ground — not the mandatory one.** The
surface hierarchy is inverted from a conventional white-page system: the warm field is the ground and
white is an *elevation*, which is a large part of why the references read as editorial rather than as
an application.

Paired with the rule that makes the default safe: **Section Background Ownership.** Every section
declares one approved background token explicitly. A section never inherits its background by
accident, and the page ground exists only beneath sections that have not declared otherwise. A
default without this becomes the only background anything ever has; this rule without a default lets
each generation drift somewhere different.

**Reversal created.** None. `tokens.json` already records *"Neutrals are warm, anchored on the #F6F5EF
page"* — this names an implemented reality and adds the declaration requirement it never had.

## DR-27 · Two `neutral-N` scales, same names, incompatible values

**Contradiction.** `variables.css`, `theme.css` and `tokens.json` carry a `neutral-N` scale whose step
numbers mean something else entirely from the canonical one.

| Legacy | Value | Canonical identity of that value |
|---|---|---|
| `--color-neutral-50` | `#F6F5EF` | `neutral-200` |
| `--color-neutral-100` | `#EFEEE7` | *(none — see DR-32)* |
| `--color-neutral-200` | `#D9D9D9` | `border-default` |
| `--color-neutral-600` | `#626262` | `text-secondary` |
| `--color-neutral-800` | `#2A2A2A` | `text-primary` |

Canonical `neutral-50` is `#FCFCFA` and canonical `neutral-200` is `#F6F5EF`. Two contradictory
`neutral-200` values in one pack is a standing trap for anyone reading either file.

**Rule it breaks.** `MANIFEST.md` — *"single source of VALUES"*. Two scales sharing a name is two
sources.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-30, by the Canonical Colour Foundation).

**Resolution.** Canonical numbering becomes the **only** authoritative neutral scale. Every canonical
value already exists in the pack — just at a different step number — so this is a renumbering, not a

The consumption order this establishes, and the reason the numbered scale is not the public API:

> **Semantic tokens → canonical tonal tokens → legacy aliases (deprecated).**
>
> Semantic tokens are the public API. Canonical tonal tokens are consumed only when *building* a
> semantic token or defining a new component. Legacy aliases are compatibility-only, and are removed
> once every consumer has migrated — not before.

**Reversal created.** Renumbers a scale in live use. The values a consumer receives are unchanged at
every migrated call site; the names' *meanings* change, which is why the aliases are mandatory rather
than a courtesy.

## DR-28 · The system declares itself light-only and the references contain dark sections

**Contradiction.** `tokens.json` declares *"Theme model: LIGHT ONLY — dark surfaces are not part of
the current Example Brand website language"* and `variables.css:89–90` marks its own dark steps
*DORMANT — dark tier, not part of the current language*. `Frame 2147226585` measures **15.2%**
`#252525`, and `Frame 2147226586` also carries dark. The canonical foundation supplies a full dark
ramp (`neutral-800/900/950/1000`).

**Rule it breaks.** The LIGHT ONLY declaration, and the DORMANT marking on the dark steps.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-30, by the Canonical Colour Foundation).

**Resolution.** This is **not** the introduction of a dark theme, and the distinction is the whole
row. It is the introduction of a **governed editorial dark surface** — one more section background,
selected for a communicative reason, never a global inversion.

> **The system remains light-first and editorial. Dark surfaces are intentional compositional
> devices, not a theme.** Dark is never used to produce a dark-mode experience.

**Permitted:** hero · closing CTA band · footer · immersive storytelling · full-bleed media
environments · product reveals · transitional narrative moments.
**Forbidden:** general content sections · forms · documentation · long-form reading · dashboards ·
arbitrary section alternation · whole-page dark.

**And a positive constraint, because rarity is what gives it weight:** a page carries **no more than
two** dark environmental sections. Dark surfaces are narrative moments, not layout defaults.

The `"LIGHT ONLY"` wording is retired — it no longer describes the system — in favour of
**"Light-first, editorial."** That tells a future contributor the four things they need: the system is
predominantly light, dark exists, dark is governed, and there is no dark theme.

**Reversal created.** Retires an absolute declaration and un-dormants a token tier. Constrained by the
permitted/forbidden lists and the ≤2 ceiling, without which "dark is allowed" would become "dark is
available".

## DR-29 · "Consume the closest semantic token" without a rule for which token is legal where

**Contradiction.** The foundation instructs that *"if a component requires colour, consume the closest
semantic token defined below"* — but several tokens are illegal in several roles by measurement, and
nothing in the system says which. `blue-500` is a legitimate surface and an illegal text colour;
`text-muted` is legitimate at large sizes and illegal as body text. "Closest" selects on hue, and the

**Rule it breaks.** `COMPOSE.md:564` promises *"(The role table lives in `foundation/COLOUR.md`; the
values live in the brand pack.)"* — a dangling cross-reference: `COLOUR.md` has no role table and

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-30, by the Canonical Colour Foundation).

**Resolution.** The promise is honoured by splitting it the way the promise itself already describes.
`COLOUR.md` receives the role table **in role form** — *the emphasis accent, the information accent* —
which is its established convention (§9 already reads *"the identity's own marker colour first, then
the secondary accent"*) and which keeps it free of hues and values. The **hue↔role binding and the
measured legality** live in the brand pack, beside the contrast table already at
`colors_and_type.css:93–113`.

Role legality is stated as measurement, not preference: which tokens are legal as body text, as large

**Reversal created.** None. This fills a table the system declared a home for and never wrote.

## DR-30 · Three accent quantities that appear to contradict each other

**Contradiction.** The foundation states the interface is *"approximately 95% Neutral, 5% Accent."*
`COMPOSE.md` §C.5 states **≤20% accent area** per section. The approved closing band
(`proof-of-work-section.png`) measures **97.8% chromatic**. Read as one flat rule, all three cannot
hold, and the reference band would be illegal under either of the first two.

**Rule it engages.** `COMPOSE.md` §C.5's accent budget; `COLOUR.md` §16's floor.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-30, by the Canonical Colour Foundation).

**Resolution.** The three are not in conflict — they are three different **scopes**, and the reference
measurements establish which is which:

| Scope | Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Page aggregate | ~5% accent / 90–95% neutral | the frames measure 0.8–18.7% chromatic, mean ≈8% |
| Per section | ≤20% area, ≤3 moments, one dominant | `COMPOSE.md` §C.5 + DR-1 |
| A **declared** accent-surface section | exempt from the area budget | the closing band measures 97.8% |
| Page floor | at least one accent moment somewhere | `COLOUR.md` §16 |

The exemption is the load-bearing addition: without it, stating 95/5 makes the approved closing band a
violation. It is available only to a section that *declares* itself an accent surface — an exemption
that applies silently is not an exemption, it is a hole.

The **floor** is recorded here because it is currently unenforceable: `COLOUR.md` §16 states *"A page
with no accent anywhere is a failure this discipline must be able to state, or the ceiling is the only
rule it has"* — and no harness implements it. A page with zero accents passes every colour check
today. So does the ≤20% area budget go unenforced (`verify.py:612` — *"The ≤20% budget governs area
and cannot see scattering"*). Both are checks that cannot fail, and both are implemented as part of
this work.

**Reversal created.** None. It scopes three rules that were already compatible and adds the exemption
that makes the approved reference legal.

## DR-31 · `surface-N` collides with a prefix already carrying two other kinds of token

**Contradiction.** The canonical foundation defines `surface-1…5` as tonal steps. The pack's
`--cr-surface-*` prefix is already occupied twice over:

| Existing | Kind | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| `-base` / `-rest` / `-contrast` / `-figure` | **role** tokens | 36 · 9 · 11 · 0 |
| `-image` / `-focus` / `-photographic` / `-scrim` | **mechanism** — a URL, a position, an opacity, a blend | throughout |

And `tokens.json` carries an incompatibly-numbered `surface` tier of its own: `surface.0 #F6F5EF`,
`.1 #EFEEE7`, `.2`/`.3 #FFFFFF`, `.accent #26B7FF`.

**Rule it engages.** The naming discipline — one prefix, one kind of thing.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-30, by the Canonical Colour Foundation).

**Resolution.** The canonical `--cr-surface-1…5` API is adopted **verbatim**. The overloaded party is
the *existing* namespace, not the canonical vocabulary, so the collision is resolved by moving the
incumbents — never by renaming the supplied API.

> **Adopt the canonical foundation; do not reinterpret it.** An integration that improves the thing it
> is integrating has stopped integrating.

**Measured first, because it changes the scope.** `--cr-surface-1…5` **do not exist** in the pack, so
adopting the canonical API creates **no functional key collision** — nothing is overwritten and nothing
resolves differently. The overload is a readability problem, not a correctness one, and that
distinction sets how much of it this row authorizes.

| Incumbent | Resolution | Authorized here |
|---|---|---|
| `--cr-surface-base` (`#F6F5EF`) | deprecated alias → `--cr-surface-4` — **already the same value** | **yes** |
| `--cr-surface-rest` (`#FFFFFF`) | deprecated alias → `--cr-surface-1` — **already the same value** | **yes** |
| `--cr-surface-contrast` (blue) | it is an **accent**, not a neutral surface — re-pointed to the governed accent background; alias retained | **yes** |
| `--cr-surface-figure` (0 uses) | deprecated in place | **yes** |
| `-image` / `-focus` / `-photographic` / `-scrim` / `-fit` | belong under a mechanism namespace, not a colour one | **no — deferred** |
| `tokens.json surface.0…3` | renumbered onto canonical | **yes** |

The four role tokens are **read-only consumers**, so re-pointing them is safe and their old names keep
working. The two heaviest already hold their canonical values, making that half a renumbering rather
than a re-colouring.

**Why the mechanism tokens are deferred rather than moved.** They are *written* by component rules, not
merely read — 46 setters in the pack and 13 further references outside it. **A CSS custom property
alias cannot cover a setter**: a rule that assigns `--cr-surface-image` writes that exact key, so a
rename is a 59-site edit to the environmental-imagery machinery, none of which is a colour change.
Bundling it here would mean a pass in which a colour regression and an imagery regression are
indistinguishable — the same reason DR-22 separated visual migration from contract migration. They stay
where they are, correctly named for what they do but filed under the wrong prefix, until a pass that
owns imagery moves them.

**Reversal created.** None. The canonical API is added to a prefix that had no conflicting keys, and
four read-only role tokens are re-pointed with their names retained. **Recorded as still open:** the
mechanism namespace remains overloaded, and this row does not pretend otherwise.

## DR-32 · A value in live use that the canonical set does not contain

**Contradiction.** `#EFEEE7` — the quiet inset step — is live, consumed as `--cr-bg-tint`,
`--cr-bg-warm-2` and `--cr-bg-grey-100`, and as the fallback for four re-pointed legacy ramps. It is
**not in the canonical foundation at all**. The foundation is closed and immutable, so the value

**Rule it breaks.** *"All future pages, sections, templates, components, specimens and generated
experiences must consume these exact tokens"* — while a live token consumes something else.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-30, by the Canonical Colour Foundation).

**Resolution.** Re-pointed to **`neutral-300` `#ECEBE3`**, its nearest canonical neighbour (manhattan

never generate alternatives.* A canonical set that grows a step whenever an existing value does not
fit is not canonical.

## DR-46 · A photograph beneath composed content

**Contradiction.** DR-7 resolved that *"a photograph is a **subject**, never a surface"*, retired the veil
ramp and the scrim tokens, and re-catalogued two environment assets as subjects on the grounds that one was
*"too detailed to sit behind copy."* A reconciliation pass has since directed that abstract gradients and
decorative graphics be replaced with editorial nature photography, that **photography behave as architecture**
with the layout wrapping around it. Five of the ten modernized specimens now carry environmental photography
beneath composed content, and one of them uses the very asset DR-7 named. DR-7 and the artifact disagree.

**Compounding it, and recorded rather than glossed:** those five edits were made **before** this row existed,
which is itself a breach of the Governance Stability Rule — *"add the row before the edit, never after."* The
edits are not reversed by that breach, but the sequence is part of the record.

**Rule it breaks.** DR-7, directly and in its central clause. Also `rulebooks/creatives.md` (the hero's
typography occupies the negative space), `component-governance/COMPOSE.md` §A2.2 (Immersive Hero forbidden
states), `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §1.1, `foundation/ILLUSTRATION.md`, and the two ASSETS.md catalogue
entries DR-7 rewrote.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-07-31, by the Section Modernization Program).

**Resolution.** DR-7 was protecting a measurable property — **type must never draw its legibility from a
photograph** — and enforced it with a categorical ban because a categorical ban needs no measurement. The
property is kept and the ban is narrowed to it:

> **A photograph occupies a position the composition allocates to it. It is never applied to a surface that
> also carries type.**
>
> **The test — remove the photograph.** If the type's legibility is unchanged, the photograph was a subject
> and is permitted. If the type becomes unreadable, or was readable only because a scrim, veil, overlay or
> opacity sat on top of the photograph, the photograph was a surface and is refused.

The test is decidable by an author *before* authoring and by a machine *after*: **no text may draw its
contrast from a photographic ancestor.** Text whose nearest opaque painted ancestor is the photographic
element itself is a violation; text on an opaque panel that happens to overlay a photograph is not.

**This keeps the accessibility floor by construction rather than by measurement** — there is no
text-on-photograph case left to measure — which is precisely why DR-7 could retire the veil ramp, and why
that retirement **survives untouched**. A scrim remains a violation rather than a fallback; a composition
that needs one has failed the test, and the answer is to move the type onto its own surface, never to veil
the photograph. `.cr-creative`'s own declaration — *"NEVER scrimmed, even when the creative it shows is an
environment"* — is the pack already saying this.

Two consequences follow. The **selection rule is unchanged**: a section selects an approved environment, it
never supplies a URL (`rulebooks/creatives.md`). And **`env-massif-clear` and `env-lake-mirror` return to the
approved library as ordinary selections** — DR-7's re-cataloguing reasoned about the behind-copy case, and
that case no longer exists.

**Reversal created.** Yes, and it is narrow. DR-7's categorical clause is superseded; its veil/scrim
retirement, its redefinition of `immersive-hero`, and its rule that film and grain on a creative-as-subject
survive untouched all stand. A reader who ports the new rule and leaves DR-7's first sentence standing will
port a contradiction — the sentence is what changed.

## DR-49 · The page inset nobody authored

**Contradiction.** Twenty of the seventy-nine modernized specimens rendered their section inset **8px from

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-08-03, on direction).

**Resolution.**

> **A section is edge-to-edge. `html, body { margin: 0 }` is mandatory in every specimen; the inset from the
> viewport is the authored page gutter and nothing else.**

The gutter is a design decision and belongs to the section. The body margin is a browser default that
predates the design system and expresses nothing. Where a preview deliberately floats an atom on the page
ground — the three atom previews do — that inset is authored as `var(--cr-space-s)` **padding** on the body,

**Reversal created.** None to the frozen library and none to the render controls. The `_v2/` controls keep the

**Verification.** Measured, not asserted: computed `body` margin read at 1440 / 1024 / 390 across all

**Left open.** `verify-consistency.py` has no page-level predicate. Until it does, this rule is enforced by

## DR-50 · Three chips where the section had one thing to say

**Contradiction.** `team-about` opens with the words *Who* / *We* / *Are* set in three sibling `<span>`s, and

**Rule it breaks.** The eyebrow is a single utterance and the chip is the unit of a discrete label — three

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-08-04, on direction).

**Resolution.**

> **The eyebrow is one tag. Where the source splits a single label across sibling elements, the chip is drawn

Made in presentation and not by deleting two spans: the DOM, its order and its element types are frozen

**Reversal created.** None. The frozen exemplar and the `_v2/` render control are untouched, so the control
still shows three chips — that is the comparison baseline behaving correctly, not a divergence to fix.

**Verification.** Predicate 6 was demonstrated to fail on this exact change **before** it was declared: the

**Left open.** Nothing gate-side knows that an eyebrow is one utterance; this row and the specimen carry it.

## DR-51 · A photograph is permitted there; that is not a reason for it

## DR-52 · The dark band becomes a wash

**Contradiction.** Four specimens — `hero-bento`, `results-proof`, `cta-banner`, `resources-carousel` —
resolved their environment against a **flat opaque band** with a hard edge across the card. That edge is what
DR-46 produced: the type was moved off the photograph onto a solid surface, and the join between picture and
surface was left as a line. Direction has since called the hard edge wrong and asked for the wash the v2 files
carried — *"0% at the top to 100% at the bottom"* — in the current palette rather than the near-blacks v2
used.

That is a **reversal of a rule this register wrote**, not an oversight, and three documents say the opposite of
what is now built:

| document | the standing wording |
|---|---|
| `governance/website/OVERVIEW.md` §172 | *"A band carries **exactly one surface** — never blended, never gradient-mixed."* |
| `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §1.1 | *"One surface per band — never blended, never gradient-mixed."* |
| `component-governance/components.md` §393 | *"The surface is SOLID — a flat brand colour, never an image or a gradient."* |

**Status.** **Resolved in the specimens** (2026-08-04, on direction). **The three documents are NOT yet
amended** — see *Left open*.

**Resolution.**

> **An environment resolves into its band through a wash, not across an edge. The wash is the page's own dark
> ink at increasing strength — never a second colour — and it finishes above the band, so the band it meets is
> the same ink and there is no seam.**

Three things make it a rule rather than four gradients:

1. **Built from one token.** Every stop is `color-mix(in srgb, var(--cr-bg-dark) N%, transparent)`. The wash
   is the direction's own `#2A2A28` at a strength, mixed toward transparent rather than toward a second
   colour, so it cannot drift off the palette and no literal enters any file. The stale `brand-gradient`
   recipe in `ASSETS.md` is **not** used: it names hexes whose tokens have since re-pointed, and it now
   resolves to near-black → near-black → green.
2. **Declared on the surface that carries the environment, never on the band.** Three of the four bands are
   assembled from sibling elements because the DOM is frozen. A gradient does not span siblings — put one on
   each and the ramp restarts at every child, drawing exactly the seams the solid band existed to avoid.
3. **The band keeps its opaque surface.** This is not a contradiction: the wash has already reached
   `--cr-bg-dark` above the band, so the two are the same ink and meet without an edge. The band is also what
   **occludes** what sits behind it — made transparent, `hero-bento`'s floating system table showed straight
   through the caption at 390 and measured **1.0:1**, white type on the white panel. A band is a surface, not
   only a colour.

**The ramp finishes above the band, and where that is, is measured.** It is not a taste value. It follows the
band's own share of the card at every viewport, and it moves at the breakpoint where that share changes —
`hero-bento` at 879, `cta-banner` at 640. Run the ramp to 100% instead and the top line of each band sits
mid-wash on a lit photograph. **Measured before the stops were set:** `cta-banner`'s headline **2.69:1** and
its second line **3.59:1**; `results-proof`'s stat label **3.56:1**; `hero-bento` at 390 **2.92:1**. All under
AA, and all of it the precise failure DR-46 exists to prevent.

**How long the wash runs is set by how much picture sits above the band — not by a house value.**
`cta-banner` has two fifths of its card free above the type, so its wash is a long ramp and reads as
atmosphere. `hero-bento`'s left tile has a **portrait whose subject runs to within a few pixels of the band**,
so the same long ramp put 85% ink across the face — it satisfied the join and lost the thing the tile is of.
Human imagery is a subject here and is never substituted (DR-46), so that tile's wash holds light through the
face and does its darkening in the last eighth: a feathered join rather than a ramp. **This is one rule, not
two.** A wash resolves the picture into the band; a composition with no picture left to resolve gets a short
one. The cost is stated rather than hidden — the lowest part of that portrait is softer than it was, and that
is the price of removing the hard edge from a crop that never left room for one.

**Reversal created.** DR-46's *conclusion* for these four sections is reversed — the hard-edged solid band was
its visible form. **DR-46's rule itself is untouched and still holds:** no surface carries both a photograph
and type. Every line of type in all four still sits on flat `--cr-bg-dark`; what changed is how the card
arrives there. DR-7's scrim retirement is likewise untouched — a scrim is a veil laid under type to rescue it,
and there is no type in the washed region.

**Verification.** Rendered contrast of every line in all four bands, sampled from the real composite under the
glyphs — not predicted from an alpha stop — at 1440 / 1024 / 390. **All pass AA after the change**; the
lowest is 8.54:1 and most are 14.38:1. The single remaining sub-7 reading (`results-proof`'s fourth column at
5.58:1) is on the plain card on the page ground, is unrelated to this row, and predates it. All seven
migration predicates pass on all four specimens with no new budget line, because a background is not geometry.

**Left open — and this is the important half.** The three documents above still say *never gradient-mixed*
and *never a gradient*, and the specimens now say otherwise. **Governance is not amended to accommodate
shipped work**, so this row records the contradiction rather than silently resolving it: the wording needs a
direction pass at all three altitudes, in one sweep, phrased as *what a band may resolve through* rather than
patched section by section. Until that pass, the four specimens are **knowingly ahead of the documents that
govern them**, and any reader comparing the two should trust this row for the intent and the documents for
everything a wash does not touch.

(6 marks). Retrieval returns the markup and the content never arrives, so a page composing one of
them renders a heading and an empty column.

**Two of those five were selected on merit for a real brief and then withdrawn** once the emptiness
was found. The catalog advertises 64 selectable sections and delivers 61.

The obstacle is not the emptiness; it is that the repair is forbidden. **Predicate 3 (SCRIPT
IDENTITY)** requires every `<script>` to be byte-identical to the frozen original, so moving content
out of a script and into the markup fails the migration gate — and `MIGRATION.md` recorded the debt
without a route to paying it: *"The three JS-authored sections are migrated **as far as CSS reaches**,
which was the stated limit."*

**Rule it breaks.** The section contract in `section-library/README.md`, continuously, for every page
that cannot compose these five. And the lifecycle rule in the same file — *"a section that has not
passed verification is not in the catalog"* — which cannot hold while a section can be catalogued,
retrievable and empty.

**Status.** **Resolved** (2026-08-05).

**Resolution.** DR-22's Pilot Constraint is not reopened. It says *"no **behavioural contract** may
change"*, and predicate 3 is a **proxy** for that: byte-identical scripts cannot have changed
behaviour. The gate already carries the **direct** measure — **predicate 1 compares the two sides on
the hydrated DOM**, after scripts have run, precisely because *"a static tag-tree diff passes
trivially on exactly the files where the constraint is hardest to keep."*

> **A script may be relieved of content it was only printing, provided the hydrated DOM is unchanged.**
> The exemption is not granted on trust: it holds only while **predicate 1** shows the rendered result
> identical and **predicate 4** shows every class the script drives still present. Both already run.
> A script that changes what it *does* — a new listener, a different state machine, a changed
> transition — still fails predicate 3 as before.

This is the proxy yielding to the direct measurement in the one case where they can disagree, and only
there. Where predicate 1 cannot vouch for the change, predicate 3 stands unchanged.

**Reversal created.** Narrow, and it is a narrowing rather than a widening: predicate 3 stops
asserting byte-identity for the single transformation the section contract requires, and asserts it
for everything else. DR-22's constraint is unchanged; what changes is that it is now enforced by the
measurement that can actually see behaviour rather than by a hash that cannot tell content from code.

**Verification.** Negative-controlled from both sides: a script edit that alters the hydrated DOM must
still fail, and a script edit that adds behaviour must still fail. Demonstrated, not asserted.

**Left open.** `faq-split` carries a **pre-existing** stale budget claim under predicate 6
(`.a-body | font-size | type-ramp` claims a change that did not happen). It predates this row, is
unrelated to it, and is not repaired here — recorded so the next reader does not attribute it to this
change.
