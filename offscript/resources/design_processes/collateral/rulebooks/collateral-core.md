# Collateral-Core Rulebook — layout, composition & structure governance

## What this document is

The **brand-invariant governance for collateral *structure*** — how a page is composed, how the
eye is led through it, and how a multi-page piece holds together as one family. It is the layer
beneath the philosophy and above the brand pack: the **PHILOSOPHY** sets the laws of comprehension,
this rulebook makes the **structural** ones decidable, and the **brand pack** supplies the concrete
values (margins, column counts, the spacing scale, type sizes) those rules reference *by role*.

**Authority:** `PURPOSE.md` → `PHILOSOPHY.md` → **this rulebook** → component governance. On any
conflict the philosophy and purpose win.

**The test every line passes:** *would this still be true after a complete rebrand?* Nothing here
names a colour, a typeface, a measurement, a class, or a brand. Every concrete number — the outer
margin, the column maximum, the spacing scale, the inter-block rhythm, the body-size floor — lives in
the **brand pack** and is referenced here only by its role.

---

## 1. The structured editorial grid — the foundation

> *(The brand pack may name a specific tradition for this grid — Swiss, editorial, or otherwise. That
> name is a brand value; the rules below are the rebrand-invariant discipline beneath it.)*

Collateral is composed on a disciplined editorial grid. The structure itself does much of the
communicating before a word is read.

- **The grid is the foundation.** Every element aligns to a shared grid and a shared spacing scale;
  nothing is placed by eye. Alignment is the cheapest, strongest signal of order.
- **Spacing is modular.** Distances come from one consistent scale, not ad-hoc values. A repeated
  rhythm reads as intentional; arbitrary gaps read as noise.
- **Hierarchy is built from scale, weight, and space** — not from decoration. What is largest,
  heaviest, and most surrounded by space is understood first. Emphasis is spent, not sprinkled.
- **Whitespace is active material.** Emptiness is used deliberately to group, to separate, and to set
  pace. It is a tool, never leftover space to be filled.
- **One idea per region.** Each region does a single job and gives the eye one unambiguous focus.
  Two co-equal ideas competing for one region is a hierarchy not yet decided.
- **Restraint is the default.** Complexity must be earned by the information; the resting state is
  spare, ordered, and quiet.

---

## 2. Composition & layout

- **One major component per row.** A row holds a single component, *or* a homogeneous peer set (a
  group of like stats, a set of equal feature cards, one comparison). Distinct components never sit
  side by side; any asymmetry stays *inside* a single component, never between two.
- **Everything lives within and aligns to the margins.** Content respects the page frame; the only
  sanctioned edge-to-edge treatments are the ones the system explicitly defines (a full-bleed cover, a
  deliberately bled band). Nothing floats past the margin by accident.
- **Columns are equal and few.** Prefer one or two columns; allow a small fixed maximum of equal
  columns for homogeneous peer sets. Unequal "sidebar" splits are not part of the system — asymmetric
  emphasis is achieved through scale and space, not through off-ratio columns.
- **Reading flow is top-to-bottom.** A page is travelled from the header downward in one continuous
  path; the layout makes that path obvious without instruction.
- **The structure should be self-evident.** Prefer the arrangement from which a reader can *infer* the
  relationships — what groups with what, what follows what — without being told.

---

## 3. The constant frame, the varying composition

A multi-page piece reads as a family because the **frame never changes** while the **composition
always does**.

- **The frame is invariant:** every page carries the same furniture in the same place — an identifying
  header at the top and a thin footer line at the bottom. This consistency is what makes a set of pages
  feel like one document.
- **The composition varies page to page.** The lead layout between header and footer changes for each
  page — a list, a peer grid, a comparison, a statement, a stat treatment, a quote. **Sameness of
  *language* is the goal; sameness of *layout* is the failure.** A deck where every page repeats the
  same shape with the words swapped is template-cloning, the cardinal failure mode.
- **Vary the layout, never the language.** Each page is composed freshly for its own content, yet every
  page obeys the same structural discipline, so the family resemblance holds.

---

## 4. Vertical fill — no voids, no dead zones

A page must read **complete** — filled top to bottom with intent, never a thin component floating in
emptiness, never a hole in the middle, never a dead band above the footer.

- **Only the footer may pin to the bottom.** Content flows downward from the header in one consistent
  gap rhythm. A content block must never be anchored to the page floor to "balance" the page — that
  opens a void above it. The single element that settles against the bottom margin is the thin footer.
- **Fill through scale and distributed spacing, not through cramming.** A page is made to feel full by a
  commanding headline, a lead component sized to carry the page, and one repeated inter-block gap —
  *not* by adding words or concentrating all the slack into one large gap.
- **If a page feels empty,** enlarge the headline, give the lead component more room, or add one more
  peer item — never drop a band to the floor to fill space.

---

## 5. Density balance

- **Complete, never sparse; purposeful, never crowded.** Each region carries the amount of information
  the idea requires — no padding into emptiness, no cramming into illegibility.
- **The page has a ceiling and a floor of content.** Too little reads as unfinished; too much risks
  overrunning the fixed page. The concrete per-region budgets (how many items a lead component holds,
  how many words a block carries, the safe component sizes) are **brand-pack / format values**,
  calibrated to the page dimensions — referenced here only as "respect the format's density budget."
- **When content will not fit, cut — never shrink below the legibility floor** and never collapse the
  structural discipline (the consistent frame, the box model) to win space.

---

## 6. Margin & alignment discipline

- The outer margin is **identical on every page** (its value lives in the brand pack). It is the quiet
  constant that frames the whole document.
- Everything aligns to the margin and to the grid; ragged, near-but-not-aligned edges are a defect, not
  a style.
- Edge-to-edge is reserved for the explicitly sanctioned full-bleed treatments; everywhere else, the
  margin is inviolate.

---

## 7. Imagery & emphasis, used with restraint

- **Imagery is concentrated where it does the most communicative work, never scattered as decoration.**
  The system decides *where* imagery belongs and holds the line; interior interest comes primarily from
  composition, hierarchy, scale, tonal surfaces, and space. (The specific placement convention — which
  surfaces carry photography — is a brand-pack decision; the invariant is *restraint and intent*.)
- **Depth and emphasis come from the system's chosen visual language, not ad-hoc devices.** No emphasis
  treatment is invented page-by-page; a region is emphasised only through the means the system already
  defines (scale, weight, a sanctioned surface). Decorative one-off devices are not introduced.
- **Visual complexity is permitted only in proportion to the meaning it carries** — never as expression
  for its own sake.

---

## 8. Failure modes this rulebook exists to prevent

- **Template cloning** — every page the same shape with the words changed.
- **Mid-page voids and bottom dead-zones** — content anchored to the floor, slack pooled into one gap.
- **Components sharing a row** — distinct components placed side by side, breaking one-per-row.
- **Filling space** — elements added to occupy a region rather than to communicate.
- **Off-grid / off-margin drift** — content that ignores the shared grid, scale, or margin.
- **Sparse pages** — a lone thin component adrift in whitespace, reading as unfinished.

> The final structural test for any page: **does its composition lead the reader cleanly from the
> header to the next step, with one idea per region, a consistent frame, and no wasted or crowded
> space — and does it differ in layout from its neighbours while sharing their discipline?**
