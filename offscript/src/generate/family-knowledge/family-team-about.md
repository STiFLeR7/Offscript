# Team / about

The people role: building trust in who stands behind the offering.

## Purpose
Build trust in who stands behind the offering — making an organization legible and human.

## Mission
Show there are real, credible people behind the promise — durable across any rebrand.

## Information Density
Moderate: portraits with names and roles, optionally story copy.

## Reading Behaviour
Scanned for faces and roles; read more closely where story copy is present.

## Interaction Style
Static; it presents people, it does not interact.

## Decision Style
Chosen when the people are part of the credibility argument; a portrait grid for a broad team, a formal row for leadership, a minimal row for a small team, a team-plus-story hybrid on richer about pages.

## Strengths
Makes an organization legible and human, converting an abstract promise into people who own it.

## Weaknesses
Adds nothing — and reads as filler — when it shows decorative head-count rather than real names and responsibilities.

## Avoid When
When the people are not part of the credibility argument — reach for another proof role instead.

## Typical Inputs
Genuine portraits of actual team members, with real names and real responsibilities; optional story.

## Typical Outputs
A reader assured there are real, credible people behind the promise.

## Sibling Differences
A portrait grid for a broad team; a formal leadership row for executives; a minimal founders row for a small team; a team-plus-story hybrid on richer about pages.

## Escalation Rules
Real people only — never invented faces, stock stand-ins, or fabricated roles; credibility from specificity, not decorative head-count; no claim without proof.

## Family Character
Human and credible — the legible face of the organization.

## Conversion Style
Builds trust in the people, supporting the ask rather than making it.

## Expected Visitor State
A reader assessing whether real, credible people stand behind the offering.
