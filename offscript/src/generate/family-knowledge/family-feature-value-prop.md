# Feature / value-prop

The explanatory core: making a capability or value understood, one idea at a time.

## Purpose
The explanatory core that makes a capability or value understood — one idea at a time, showing rather than only telling.

## Mission
Turn the offering into understanding the reader can hold — the durable persuasion responsibility, independent of brand.

## Information Density
Moderate and shaped to one idea per band; layers of detail are offered but the surface always stands on its own.

## Reading Behaviour
Read for comprehension, scanned first for the gist; deeper layers are opted into, never forced.

## Interaction Style
Mostly static showing; where features are a sequence or parallel set, a switcher or progressive reveal lets the reader opt into detail.

## Decision Style
Chosen whenever a capability must be understood; the shape follows the content's structure — peers, one dominant, progressive, sequential, long-form, "also", or single manifesto.

## Strengths
Explains and proves a value by showing it, and flexes to whatever shape the content's structure demands.

## Weaknesses
Loses its point if it carries two messages at once, or if equal weight is given where ranks actually differ.

## Avoid When
When a number — not a description — is the strongest argument (reach for a stats/outcomes role), or when the items are a true ordered sequence (reach for a process role).

## Typical Inputs
One idea, its supporting points (true peers, or one dominant item), and optionally a closing nudge.

## Typical Outputs
A reader who understands what the offering does and why it is worth their time.

## Sibling Differences
Equal cards when items are true peers; a mixed-size set when one item should dominate; an expandable list for progressive detail opted into; a switcher when features are a sequence or parallel use-cases; a pinned or long-form walk for storytelling on long pages; a secondary "also" set after the main one; a single-statement reveal for one manifesto-grade idea.

## Escalation Rules
One idea per component; peers shown as peers and a dominant item given dominant weight; detail offered, never forced; heavy-motion or full-length shapes at most once per page and only on long pages; progressive detail kept off the opening screen; one focal point.

## Family Character
Explanatory and substantive — the page's argument advancing, calm enough to be understood, structured to the idea it carries.

## Conversion Style
Advances the argument by building understanding; it persuades toward the eventual ask rather than making it.

## Expected Visitor State
A reader past orientation who is deciding whether the offering does something worth their time.
