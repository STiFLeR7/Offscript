# Hero

The opening role: the first screen that states one promise and offers one next step.

## Purpose
The opening screen that states the single most important promise and gives the page its one clear next step, in the few seconds before the reader decides whether to grant the scroll.

## Mission
Win the scroll by making one promise legible and one action obvious — the durable opening responsibility, true of any brand.

## Information Density
Deliberately sparse: one promise, one primary action, and at most a single proof or product artifact. It makes one argument legible, never a feature list.

## Reading Behaviour
Glanced, not studied — read in seconds. The reader decides from it whether to continue at all.

## Interaction Style
Read, not operated. It carries exactly one primary action; any secondary option is visibly subordinate.

## Decision Style
Chosen for the opening band of nearly every page; the shape is then chosen by what evidence is available to back the promise.

## Strengths
Orients a cold reader with no prior context and turns attention into a single next step better than any other role.

## Weaknesses
It spends trust if it overstates — a hero that overpromises is a debt the rest of the page cannot repay. It cannot carry detail or a feature inventory.

## Avoid When
When there is no single dominant promise to make, or when a supporting artifact would have to be faked — reach for a quieter explanatory beat instead, and never show an invented demo.

## Typical Inputs
One clear promise, one primary action, and — optionally — one genuine proof or product artifact: real tiles, a true product state, or early trust marks.

## Typical Outputs
An oriented reader who understands the one reason this matters to them and the one obvious thing to do next.

## Sibling Differences
A proof-tile opening when several real proof tiles tell the story; a calm credibility opening when cards-of-proof beneath the promise carry more weight than a busy grid; a product-state opening when a genuine artifact can be shown; a conversational/demo opening only when there is a real live-demo story to capture intent against.

## Escalation Rules
One promise, not the whole product; exactly one primary action; fully self-orienting (assumes no prior context); any shown artifact must be genuine; one focal point, and no claim without proof beside it.

## Family Character
A confident, assertive opening that signals the page's single argument. It is the orientation itself, so it stands entirely on its own.

## Conversion Style
Opens the argument and sets the page's one next step. It promises, and the body must repay — conversion is invited here but earned later.

## Expected Visitor State
A first-contact reader with no prior context, deciding in seconds whether this is worth their attention.
