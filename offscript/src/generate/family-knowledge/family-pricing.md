# Pricing

The cost role: making the price of saying yes clear and low-anxiety.

## Purpose
Make the cost of saying yes clear and low-anxiety, so price never becomes the silent reason a ready reader leaves.

## Mission
Let a reader know what it costs and what they get before they commit — a responsibility that holds across any rebrand.

## Information Density
Structured and comparable: a set of plan or package tiles, optionally a billing toggle and folded-in reassurance.

## Reading Behaviour
Scanned for fit then compared closely; the reader looks for the plan that matches them.

## Interaction Style
Largely static, with an optional billing toggle; one plan may be the honest focal point.

## Decision Style
Chosen when cost is part of the decision; equal cards for parallel peers, an unequal set for offers of differing scope, a persona-split set when plans divide by group, a pricing-plus-FAQ band on short pages.

## Strengths
Lowers the cost of "yes" by making price legible and anxiety-free at the moment of decision.

## Weaknesses
Splits intent and erodes trust the moment it competes with a second price table or leans on pressure.

## Avoid When
When the question is feature parity rather than price — reach for a comparison role instead.

## Typical Inputs
The real plans or packages, their prices, and optional reassurance — trust marks or a short FAQ.

## Typical Outputs
A reader who knows what it costs and what they get, and feels safe to commit.

## Sibling Differences
Equal plan cards for parallel peers; an unequal package set for offers of differing scope; a persona-split tabbed set when plans divide by group; a pricing-plus-FAQ band where both must fit a short page.

## Escalation Rules
At most one pricing component per page; total transparency — never hide, bury, or disguise cost, no false scarcity or dark patterns; equal plans shown as peers and any emphasized plan the single honest focal point.

## Family Character
Transparent and low-anxiety — it reassures rather than pressures.

## Conversion Style
Removes price as a silent objection right before the ask; it lowers the cost of yes, never raises it with pressure.

## Expected Visitor State
A near-ready reader for whom cost is the remaining question.
