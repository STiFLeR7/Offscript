# Comparison

The adjudication role: helping a deciding reader weigh options factor by factor.

## Purpose
Help a deciding reader weigh options, factor by factor, honestly.

## Mission
Let a reader satisfy themselves this is the right choice among real alternatives — durable across any rebrand.

## Information Density
Dense but structured: a factor-by-column matrix, one factor per row, the reader ordering importance themselves.

## Reading Behaviour
Studied and cross-referenced; the reader compares rows and columns deliberately.

## Interaction Style
Static adjudication; the matrix presents, the reader judges.

## Decision Style
Chosen at the decision stage when a reader is actively choosing between this and an alternative, or between plans.

## Strengths
Earns belief precisely by being fair — admitting where this is not the best fit makes it trusted where it is.

## Weaknesses
Destroys trust faster than it wins the point if it straw-mans the alternative or hides where this loses.

## Avoid When
When the axis is purely cost and packaging (reach for a pricing role), or when the goal is to explain rather than adjudicate (reach for a feature role).

## Typical Inputs
The real alternatives or plans, and the factors that distinguish them — represented fairly.

## Typical Outputs
A reader confident they have made the right choice among real options.

## Sibling Differences
An us-versus-alternative or plan-versus-plan matrix at the decision stage; a single comparison column when composing the comparison into another band.

## Escalation Rules
Fair representation only — never a straw-man of the alternative; honesty about limits outperforms claimed perfection; one factor per row; no claim without proof beside it.

## Family Character
Candid and even-handed — it wins by fairness, not by spin.

## Conversion Style
Resolves the "is this the right choice?" doubt, moving a deciding reader toward commitment.

## Expected Visitor State
A reader actively weighing this against real alternatives.
