# Navigation

The persistent top orientation instrument of the page.

## Purpose
The persistent top instrument that answers the reader's three continuous questions — where am I, what is this, where can I go — cheaply, at every moment.

## Mission
Keep the reader oriented and free to move at any point on the page — a responsibility that holds across any rebrand.

## Information Density
Minimal and stable: a brand mark, a small set of section links, and one primary action. It is orientation, not a menu of everything.

## Reading Behaviour
Scanned in passing and returned to repeatedly; never read in sequence.

## Interaction Style
Wayfinding by legible choice — quiet links plus at most one primary action. Movement must always feel reversible.

## Decision Style
Present whenever a page has more than one destination; reach for a bare closing link set instead when only end-of-page wayfinding is needed.

## Strengths
Lets a reader act without first working out where they are, and makes the site's structure learnable once and reusable everywhere.

## Weaknesses
Adds no argument of its own; if it swells into a full menu it stops orienting and starts overwhelming.

## Avoid When
When the page has a single destination, or when the need is only end-of-page wayfinding — reach for the footer's link set instead.

## Typical Inputs
A brand mark, a small consistent set of destination links, and one primary action.

## Typical Outputs
A reader who can act from anywhere on the page and can always find their way back.

## Sibling Differences
Realized as the persistent top orientation bar. Lighter wayfinding at the page's close is the footer's link set, not this role.

## Escalation Rules
Orientation is the goal, not the menu; the same kinds of destinations live in the same kinds of places site-wide; movement stays reversible; guide by choice, never trap; at most one primary action; it must self-orient a reader who arrived anywhere.

## Family Character
A calm, constant frame — present, legible, and unobtrusive; the page's steady orientation instrument.

## Conversion Style
Carries at most one primary action and otherwise stays out of the argument's way; it enables movement rather than asking for commitment.

## Expected Visitor State
A reader who may have arrived at any point and needs, continuously, to know where they are and where they can go.
