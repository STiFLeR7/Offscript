# Integrations

The fit role: answering whether this works with what the reader already uses.

## Purpose
Answer "does this work with what I already have?" — reducing the perceived cost of adopting.

## Mission
Lower the switching cost by proving ecosystem fit — a responsibility that survives any rebrand.

## Information Density
Light to moderate: a set of connection marks, optionally one line of copy per connection.

## Reading Behaviour
Scanned for recognition; the reader looks for their own tools among the marks.

## Interaction Style
Static evidence of fit; the marks do the arguing, not interaction.

## Decision Style
Chosen when ecosystem fit is part of the decision; a grid when each connection needs explanation, a lighter marquee when the marks speak for themselves.

## Strengths
Removes "but will it fit my stack?" as a silent objection, quickly and without argument.

## Weaknesses
Becomes decoration — and does not belong — if the marks argue nothing, or if it implies a fit that is not real.

## Avoid When
When the marks signal trust rather than compatibility — reach for a social-proof role instead.

## Typical Inputs
Real, supported connection marks, optionally a line of copy per connection.

## Typical Outputs
A reader reassured that adopting this will not mean abandoning their existing tools.

## Sibling Differences
A grid when each connection needs a line of explanation; a lighter marquee when the marks speak for themselves and a quieter beat is wanted.

## Escalation Rules
Only real, supported connections — never imply an integration that does not exist; the marks must be evidence of fit, not decoration, and never a claim without proof.

## Family Character
Practical and reassuring — a quiet "it fits what you already use" bridge.

## Conversion Style
Removes an adoption objection; it clears the path to the ask rather than making it.

## Expected Visitor State
A reader weighing adoption who is wondering whether this fits their existing tools.
