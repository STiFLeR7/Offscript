# HANDOFF-WEBSITE — Operating the Website Track with Offscript

> **Who this is for:** the next block in our working system — whoever picks up the **Website track** after us.
> **What it is:** a plain-language transfer of *our operating conditions* for websites — the philosophy, the rules, the way work flows, and what "done" looks like. Read it once and you can brief Offscript for a website without re-deriving any of this.
> **What it is *not*:** a technical manual, and not a brand sheet. There is no code here, nothing to install, and **no concrete brand inside** — colours, type, voice and assets all arrive from whichever brand is currently plugged in. This describes the **engine and its conditions**, expressed in pseudo terms on purpose, so it survives a brand swap.
>
> **Where it sits.** Our system runs three tracks — **Website** (active, this document), **Collateral** (active, its own handoff), **Pitch Deck** (blocked, parked until its governance lands). This handoff covers Website only. The tracks share brand DNA; they differ in *how a person moves through the result*.

---

## 1. The big picture in one line

> **The design team authors the system → Offscript assembles the page within the system's rails → out comes an on-brand, self-contained website page, ready to hand off.**

```
   THE AUTHORED SYSTEM            OFFSCRIPT (the generator)            THE OUTPUT
   ─────────────────────────     ─────────────────────────        ─────────────────────
   Website philosophy,     ──▶    Reads the brief + philosophy,    A single, self-contained
   brand law, voice,              pulls from the catalog by         on-brand web page —
   do/don'ts, a component         intent, assembles in bands,       complete on its own,
   catalog, exemplars             checks itself against the rails   checked, ready to hand off
```

Two ideas the next block must hold onto:

1. **The authored system is the only source of truth.** Offscript never invents the brand. It works *from* what the system gives it and *checks itself against* the same thing. If something isn't in the system, Offscript shouldn't be guessing it.
2. **The brand is a plug-in.** Everything below is the *website engine*. The specific look and voice come from the installed brand. Swap the brand and the page re-themes — **the conditions in this document do not change.**

---

## 2. The Website Philosophy (What / Why / How)

Every track has its own philosophy, because each medium moves a person differently. This is ours.

**What it is.** A website is a **fluid, scrolling surface that people explore freely.** Unlike a fixed page or a linear deck, a visitor can **arrive anywhere**, scroll up or down, skim, and leave at any point. You don't control the order; you control the *invitation*.

**Why it exists / who it's for.** It exists to **guide without forcing, and to reward scrolling.** The reader showed up with a question or a doubt; each screen they pass should move them one honest step closer to understanding and acting. They should never feel railroaded — but they should never feel lost either.

**How it carries the brand — the working conditions:**
- **The section is the unit of work; the page is the narrative.** Offscript thinks at both levels at once: each section does one job, and the *order and rhythm* of sections tell a story.
- **One communication objective per section.** If two messages compete equally inside a band, split them or subordinate one.
- **One focal point per viewport.** A reader should be able to name the single most important thing on screen within about three seconds. If they can't, the hierarchy is too flat — strengthen it with scale, position, spacing and contrast.
- **Guide by rhythm, not by force.** Sections are separated by **generous whitespace and the identity mark that brackets a section** — not by drawing lines, and not by alternating the background. The page breathes; it doesn't fence.
- **Reward the scroll.** Every section must earn its place by advancing the narrative. Nothing is there to fill space.
- **Clarity first, impression second.** When a trade-off arises, understanding wins over visual complexity. The page reads first and impresses second.

> Keep any website brief written in this spirit: *"a calm, credible page that lets a sceptical buyer arrive mid-scroll and still feel oriented, then earn one clear next step."* That's a philosophy-shaped brief. "Hero headline 64px, blue button" is not.

## 3. How Offscript builds a website (the operating loop)

When Offscript is asked for a page, it runs four plain steps. This is the loop the next block inherits:

1. **Understands the request.** Reads the brief and the website philosophy to grasp *what* the page must communicate and *why* — then plans the page as a **sequence of bands**, each with a single objective.
2. **Adapts to the brand.** Pulls the right surfaces, type, spacing, voice and components **from the installed system only.** Nothing comes from outside it.
3. **Assembles — select or curate, never generate.** For each band, Offscript either **selects** an existing component that fits the intent, or **curates** a new one from existing pieces when nothing fits. It does **not** free-hand generic markup. (See §8.)
4. **Checks itself against the rails.** It measures the result against the system's rules. A section that can't be traced back to the system — neither a pulled component nor a deliberately-composed-and-marked new one — is **rejected, not quietly shipped.** Anything Offscript can't safely resolve on its own is **flagged for a human**, never hidden.

**The one idea under all four:** *Offscript generates **within rails**.* The rails are the rules. Offscript is free to be creative **inside** them and cannot step outside them. That's how the same system produces both **variety** and **consistency**.

---

## 4. The Golden Rule — no hardcoding 🚫

The single most important condition. **Describe intent and rules, never fixed one-off values.** The moment a fixed number or a one-spot instruction is baked in, the result can't adapt, can't stay consistent, and can't be reused.

| ❌ Hardcoded (don't) | ✅ Governed intent (do) |
|---|---|
| "Make the headline 64 pixels." | "The hero headline is the most commanding type on the page — it leads everything." |
| "Use colour `#XXXXXX` for the button." | "Primary actions use the **action colour** from the palette — the one thing the eye lands on to click." |
| "Edit *this exact paragraph* on the page." | "This section presents the proof points in a scannable, comparable layout." |
| "Put 24px of space between these two." | "Major bands breathe — use the generous spacing step between them." |
| "Make this section grey." | "Separate these two bands by **shifting the surface**, because they'd otherwise blur together." |

> Rule of thumb: if the instruction would still make sense on a *different* page or a *different* brand, it's governed intent ✅. If it only makes sense for one exact spot, it's a hardcode 🚫 — restate it as a rule.

---

## 5. The two-layer habit: Value + Why

Every governed item has two halves, and the next block must supply **both**:

- **The Value** — the *what* (a colour, a scale step, a surface, a rule).
- **The Why / How** — the *intent* (why it exists, when and how to apply it in context).

> Not "the accent is teal." Instead: **"the accent is teal — used only for primary actions and key highlights, never for large fills, because it should read as a spark, not a wash."**

Without the *why*, Offscript lands "close but wrong" — the right system, the wrong application. The *why* is what makes the page feel like the brand rather than merely use its colours.

## 6. The flow: Creative Director → Website Philosophy → Offscript

The next block doesn't brief Offscript in pixels. It briefs the way a creative director briefs a studio.

```
   CREATIVE DIRECTOR  ──▶   WEBSITE PHILOSOPHY  ──▶   OFFSCRIPT
   (asks What/Why/How)      (§2 — the lens)           (assembles within the rails)
```

1. **Creative Director** — sets *taste and direction*: What are we making and what must it achieve? Why does it exist, who's it for, what should they feel and do? How should it carry the brand? **Important:** the director shapes *feel*, **not correctness** — it can ask for "confident and editorial," but it cannot override the rulebook. **The brand law wins every hard call** (colour, type, spacing, do/don'ts).
2. **Website Philosophy** (§2) — turns the director's intent into something Offscript can act on, in the language of a scrolling page.
3. **Offscript** — takes brief + philosophy and produces the page within the rails.

---

## 7. What you must give Offscript (the layered inputs)

Rulebooks alone are **not enough** — they state intent but not how to apply it. Offscript needs a **layered set of inputs**. The more complete it is, the more on-brand the page. Give every item as **Value + Why** (§5).

| Layer | What it is | For a website, this drives… |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategy** | Who and why | Positioning, audience, the one action the page exists to earn |
| **Visual identity** | The look | Palette **with usage rules**, type hierarchy, spacing/grid feel, the canonical **surfaces** (§9) |
| **Brand voice** | How it speaks | Tone, person, words it loves, words it bans — with do/don't examples |
| **Do's & Don'ts** | The guardrails | "Always…", "Never…" — the things that make or break the brand on screen |
| **Brand assets & rules** | Identity bits | Logo placement, aspect-ratio rules, and which creative assets exist — the system reserves positions for the ones that don't yet |
| **Rationale** | The *why* behind choices | Short notes so Offscript applies each rule sensibly in a new context |
| **Exemplars** | Show, don't just tell | A few **on-brand reference pages/sections** — references beat written rules alone |

> **Exemplars are special.** A handful of real, on-brand examples teach Offscript more than pages of rules — they stop "drift" (slightly-wrong colour, slightly-wrong spacing) better than words can. Supply a few.

## 8. The website component catalogue (abstract — by role, never by pixels)

These are the **building blocks Offscript assembles a page from.** The next block governs them **abstractly** — what each is *for*, when to reach for it, and the rules it must respect. Offscript realises the exact look within the rails.

**Two conditions sit over the whole catalogue:**

- **Select or curate, never generate.** Offscript either pulls a catalogued block that serves the intent, or composes a new one from existing pieces — it does not free-hand generic markup. (The rails reject anything that isn't one of those two things.)
- **Pick by intent, never by name.** Each role below is a **family with several on-brand variants.** A "feature" band might be best served by a card grid, an accordion, a bento, a sticky walkthrough, or even a testimonial layout. Offscript chooses by *what the band must do*, not by what a block is called. When several variants fit equally and on-brand, it **rotates among them for variety — never settling for a worse fit just to differ.**

| Role | Type | When to reach for it | Abstract description |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hero** | Structural / Conversion | The first thing on the page | The opening statement — the core promise + one clear primary action, in the most commanding space |
| **Navigation** | Navigational | Top of every page | Orientation and wayfinding; re-tones to the hero's surface, never changes its composition |
| **Feature / value** | Content | Explaining what the offer does | A focused unit pairing a benefit with supporting detail, often with a visual |
| **Process / how-it-works** | Content | Showing a sequence or method | A small number of ordered steps that make the "how" legible |
| **Stats / outcomes** | Proof / Content | Backing a claim with numbers | A few bold figures that make impact tangible — the numbers *are* the argument |
| **Social proof / logos** | Proof | Building trust near a decision | Testimonials, quotes, ratings, or a wall of recognisable names |
| **Testimonials / case studies** | Proof | A human, trusted voice | A named customer's words and results, in quote, slider, or story form |
| **Comparison** | Content | "Us vs the alternative" | A structured side-by-side that clarifies a choice without insulting the other option |
| **Pricing** | Conversion | Helping people choose a plan | Plans laid out comparably and scannably so the choice feels easy |
| **FAQ** | Content | Removing last-minute doubt | Real questions answered plainly, low-key, near the end |
| **Call-to-action band** | Conversion | Nudging the next step | A focused, high-contrast prompt to act |
| **Contact / lead form** | Conversion | Capturing intent | A form paired with reassurance; the page's quiet hand-raise |
| **Integrations** | Content / Proof | "It fits your stack" | The systems the offer plugs into, shown as logos or copy |
| **Team / about** | Identity / Proof | The people and the story | Portraits and narrative that put a face to the brand |
| **Resources / insights** | Content | Depth and freshness | Article or news cards that signal an active, credible voice |
| **Footer** | Structural / Navigational | Closing every page | The quiet utility zone — links, legal, secondary navigation |
| **Creative position (placeholder)** | Structural | Reserving where a visual goes | A labelled reservation of a creative asset's position — the system holds the space, people make the asset (see below) |

> **A note on creatives — this system does not make them.** Photography, illustration, artwork, product screenshots, screenshot styling, image treatments, marketing graphics and motion graphics are **created independently, by people.** Wherever a page needs one, Offscript generates a **structural placeholder** indicating its position in the layout, and decides only its **placement, dimensions, spacing, alignment and responsive behaviour**. It must **never generate or describe the creative itself** — not as an image, not as a generated interface, not as an illustrative composition, and not in prose. A placeholder is the opposite of filler: it makes an absence explicit instead of faking presence. (Full doctrine: `rulebooks/creatives.md`.)

For **every** block, the rule from §4 holds: govern by **role and rules**, never by fixed pixels. "The hero leads with the core promise and one clear action" ✅ — not "the hero headline is 64px and the button is teal" 🚫.

## 9. The website operating conditions (the laws of a scrolling page)

These are the conditions that make our output *ours*. The next block should treat them as inherited law.

**Structure & focus**
- One **communication objective per section**; one **focal point per viewport** (the 3-second test).
- Build hierarchy from **scale, position, spacing, contrast and type** — not from decoration.

**Surface & rhythm**
- There is **one page surface**, plus a raised surface for cards and panels. Sections are separated by **generous whitespace and by the identity mark that brackets a section, not by alternating surfaces and not by fencing every band with a rule.**
- A **surface change is a deliberate accent moment** — an accent-surface band earns distinct treatment because that band's argument earns it, never to break up monotony on a cadence. (A dark surface tier remains defined but **dormant**: not part of the current language, retained for a future direction.)
- A band carries **exactly one surface** — never blended, never gradient-mixed, and never a change the reader can locate inside the band.
- **Surfaces have a material.** Branded surfaces are not perfectly flat digital fields: a fixed, shared texture treatment gives them a tactile, printed, editorial character — the quality of printed matter rather than of a screen. It is **material, not decoration and not depth**: uniform across the surface, marking nothing, separating nothing. The reader should perceive a tactile surface, never an identifiable texture. It applies **because of what a surface is** — currently the closing call-to-action banner and creative surfaces — never because someone chose to apply it, and never to the page surface, cards, buttons, nav, inputs, small UI, icons or typography.
- **Surface first.** A large branded surface reads as an **environment** before it reads as a layout: the background establishes atmosphere, typography establishes hierarchy, components occupy the environment. Never the reverse — and the treatment stays secondary to the type in front of it. (Atmosphere is not hierarchy; the material still marks and separates nothing.)
- Monotony is still a failure, but it is answered with **density, scale and rhythm**, not by swapping the background.

**Editorial rhythm & measure**
- **A section opens on a deliberate rhythm:** identity mark → a **large opening space** → heading → a **tight cluster gap** → supporting copy → a generous gap → primary content. The mark *introduces* the section; the heading *begins the narrative* — two different moments, so the heading never reads as attached to the mark. The heading and its supporting copy are **one thought** and bind tightly.
- **The rule is the order, not the numbers:** opening > content > cluster, at every screen size. Spacing distributed evenly because steps exist is the failure — whitespace is part of the communication hierarchy, not the room left over.
- **The identity mark brackets its section — one at the opening, one at the close.** It is a pair, not a single mark: the top says a major movement begins, the bottom says it ends, and together they make the section read as bounded rather than as content that simply stopped. **A section either takes the bracket or takes no mark at all** — half a bracket opens something it never closes. The opening gap is larger than the closing one, deliberately: equal gaps read as a box drawn around the content. **Colour priority: orange first, then blue, then the remaining accents**, and both halves of a bracket are always the same colour.
- **The mark stays scarce** — truer of a bracket than of a single mark, since each one now costs two. A page on which every section carries one has made it meaningless.
- **Consecutive marked sections should not share a colour.** A section's closing mark sits *nearer* to the next section's opening mark than to its own opening one, so colour is what tells a reader which two belong together. Rotating through the priority order provides that alternation naturally.
- **Preserve the rhythm regardless of content length.** Short content produces a calm, open composition; it never collapses vertically.
- **Four widths, not one.** The **container** frames the page. **Layout width** — the licence to use that full container — is granted only to components that genuinely need it (comparison tables, dashboards, card grids, feature matrices). **Editorial measure** holds headlines, hero messaging and CTA headlines. **Reading measure** holds paragraphs and supporting copy. **Editorial content never inherits layout width**, and never widens just because horizontal space exists.
- **Headlines and supporting copy intentionally occupy different measures.** Supporting copy does not inherit the width of the headline; that transition *is* the section's visual hierarchy, and equalising the two to tidy it removes the hierarchy it was carrying.

**Composed sections & creative**
- **A section declares how it communicates before anything is placed in it** — an editorial split, an editorial stack, an immersive hero, a narrative flow, a comparison, a product showcase, media-first or text-first. That decision drives the shape; the shape never makes the decision.
- **Closing call-to-action bands and heroes are composed sections.** Each sets its own rhythm and the content inhabits it; content occupies an editorial portion, not the full width; the identity mark **brackets** the section, opening and closing it.
- **They compose differently, and the difference is the job.** A **hero is asymmetric** — content anchored to the alignment edge, the remaining space composed rather than split — because a hero has somewhere to point. A **closing band is centred**, content and items alike, with **equal vertical padding**, because the page is over and everything converges on one ask.
- **The failure to avoid was never centring — it was the slack rectangle:** a tall band with content adrift in the middle and nobody owning the space. It is answered by **proportion**. The closing band is the one composed section that does not take the page's section padding: a closing ask does not want to be entered, it wants to be answered. And centring the box is not centring the content — editorial copy still holds its measures, so a centred band is a narrow column of type in a wide band, never full-width copy that happens to be centred.
- **Creative-first composition.** Creative assets are first-class compositional elements: their **proportion, presence and placement are decided before the surrounding layout**. **Layout adapts to the creative; the creative never adapts to available space.** Proportion comes from a closed set of approved ratios, presence from a closed set of shares, placement from a closed set of anchors — and a placeholder carries the same three, because it represents the future creative, never the available space.

**Environmental imagery — selected, never made**
- **Where the creative exists it is selected; where it does not it is reserved.** Approved environmental photography lives in one canonical library, and it is the **only** source: never stock, never generated, never another file in the repository because nothing in the library quite fit. Selecting an approved asset is a layout decision the system makes; authoring one is not, and that has not changed. **Do not reserve a position the library can already fill** — a placeholder in front of an available asset declares an absence that is not real.
- **The asset is never modified.** Gradients, overlays, atmospheric colour, grain, texture and blend modes are applied by the system, **above an untouched image** — never baked into a file. One asset can then read two ways in two sections, and a per-page effect never gets mistaken for the material.
- **Crop, never stretch.** A real asset rarely arrives at an approved proportion, so it is cropped to one at a **governed focus that travels with the asset**. "Preserve the aspect ratio" means preserve the *creative* — it is not a licence to ship an off-vocabulary proportion. If no crop survives the ratio the composition needs, choose another asset or change the layout.
- **Choose for communication, not for beauty.** Every selection answers *why is this the correct environmental atmosphere for this section?* Heroes want negative space and depth with room for typography, and typography occupies that space rather than covering the subject. Closing bands want calm and restrained contrast — the content sits *within* the environment. Editorial backgrounds reinforce the narrative and **avoid literal visual metaphor**.
- **A photographic surface keeps the contrast measurement it replaces.** Text contrast was measured against flat colour; a photograph behind copy invalidates it, so a text-bearing photographic surface carries a **measured** veil that follows the composition — holding where content sits, releasing where the environment breathes. A creative shown as the **subject** is never veiled. Same asset, two jobs; the surface decides which.

**Restraint & depth**
- **Progressive disclosure**, not everything at once. Bands feel complete without being crowded or sparse.
- **Every component justifies its presence.** No "dashboard effect" where many elements carry equal weight.
- **There is no depth.** Whitespace, typography, scale, alignment, proportion and hairline borders do *all* of the separating. **No shadows anywhere** — no glass, no backdrop blur, no glow, no depth simulation. If something must feel more important, give it more room, more scale, or a clearer position.
- **Whitespace is the primary mechanism for creating hierarchy.** Each section communicates one primary message; competing layouts, nested cards, dense information blocks and decorative UI clusters are all failures of the same kind.

**Interaction**
- **Hover only signals a real interaction** — buttons, links, nav, controls. Static, informational content never reacts to hover.
- Reveals are short and calm; the brand should read as institutional, not playful, unless the brand says otherwise.

**Hard constraints**
- **No layout hacks.** Resolve composition through spacing, grid, alignment and structure.
- **The action colour never blends into its surroundings** — a primary action always pops against its band.

**Process conditions (how Offscript must work, not just what it makes)**
- **Pick by intent, then rotate among equals** — variety is a feature; a worse fit to "be different" is not.
- **Every built page carries a short record** — one line per section — naming the band's intent, the alternatives weighed, the choice, and *why it beat the alternative.* This record travels with the page as its audit trail.
- **The brand wins every conflict.** If a rule blocks a clearly-better design, Offscript **follows the rule and flags the tension** so the rule itself can improve — it never hardcodes an off-brand value to win the argument.
- **The brand layer is the only swappable part.** Replace the brand and the whole site re-themes; none of these conditions change.

## 10. What you get back (the output)

- **A single, self-contained page per deliverable** — complete on its own.
- **On-brand by construction** — every colour, type choice and spacing traces back to the installed system.
- **Checked against the rails** — anything Offscript couldn't safely auto-resolve is flagged for a human, never hidden; the per-section record explains every choice.
- **Ready to hand off** — a finished prototype. A development team takes it to production (hosting, analytics, etc.); that part is outside Offscript.

## 11. Who does what

| The design team (authors) | Offscript (generator) |
|---|---|
| Author the website philosophy, brand law, voice, do/don'ts, exemplars | Reads them and works strictly within them |
| Describe components and intent **abstractly** | Realises them as a real, scrolling page |
| Make the design *decisions* | Handles all the *mechanics* |
| Decide *what good looks like* | Measures the output against that and self-corrects, flagging what it can't |

The next block should **never** be asked to touch code or files. If a real decision is needed, it arrives as a plain-language choice.

## 12. Pre-handoff checklist (website)

Before briefing Offscript for a page, confirm you've supplied:

- [ ] A clear, **intent-led** brief (What / Why / How) — no pixel instructions.
- [ ] The **website philosophy** (§2) as the lens — section-as-unit, page-as-narrative, guide-don't-force.
- [ ] The **layered inputs** (§7): strategy, visual identity (incl. the canonical surfaces), voice, do's & don'ts, brand assets, rationale.
- [ ] A few **website exemplars**.
- [ ] Every value paired with its **why** (§5).
- [ ] A final scan for **hardcoding** (§4) — restate any one-off as a rule.

> If a box is unticked, expect "close but not quite." The completeness of the input is the single biggest lever on how on-brand the page comes back.
