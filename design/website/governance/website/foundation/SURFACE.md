# SURFACE — The Design Discipline of What a Surface Is Made Of

> **What this is.** The Foundation discipline that owns **the material a surface is made of** — whether
> a field the reader looks at feels like a manufactured screen or like something made, handled and
> printed. It decides which surfaces carry material, why, and how far a surface stands behind what it
> carries. Its output is not a colour, a texture file, or a treatment; it is **material intent**.
>
> **What this is not.** Not colour — what a surface *is* chromatically belongs to Colour, and this
> discipline is indifferent to which colour a surface takes. Not depth: shadow, elevation and blur are
> not material and are forbidden system-wide. Not layout: how much room a surface occupies is settled
> in `SECTION_LAYOUT.md`. Not imagery: a photograph used as a creative belongs to Illustration, and
> this discipline governs only what happens when a photograph is used as a *surface*.
>
> **The one boundary above all others.** *Surface determines what a surface is made of and how far it
> recedes behind its content. It never decides what colour that surface takes, how much space it
> occupies, what sits on it, or what it means. It governs material intent, and nothing else.*
>
> **The test every line passes.** *Would this still be true if every texture asset, every colour and
> every layout in the system were replaced, while surfaces still felt like the same kind of thing?*
>
> **Where this sits.** A Design Discipline of the Website Foundation, reasoned through after
> `EDITORIAL_ART_DIRECTION.md` — which tells it whether a moment should feel handled or clean — and
> alongside Colour, Spacing, Typography, Iconography and Illustration. **On any conflict, Governance
> wins, the Interface Composition Model's judgment is inherited, and this document wins over
> `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §1 and §1.1, which operationalize it.**

## §0 — First principles

A reader does not encounter content in the abstract. They encounter it *on something*. Before a word
is read, a field of some kind has already been perceived — and that field is never neutral, because
there is no such thing as an absence of surface. A page that made no surface decision still has one:
the default, which is a perfectly flat computed colour.

That default is the problem this discipline exists to name. A perfectly flat field is not the natural
state of a surface; it is the natural state of a *screen*. Nothing in the physical world is
uninterrupted flat colour. Paper has tooth, ink has variance, printed matter carries the trace of
having been made. Every surface a person has ever touched has a material, and a field with none reads
— correctly — as manufactured rather than made.

This matters because material is one of the few properties that operates entirely below argument. A
reader cannot be persuaded that a surface feels considered; they either perceive it or they do not,
and they perceive it before they have decided to evaluate anything. Material is therefore not
decoration applied to a finished surface. It is part of what the surface *is*, and it is doing its
work before the content it carries has been read.

The discipline exists because that work is otherwise ungoverned. Material left to preference gets
applied where someone thought it would look good, which produces an identifiable texture in some
places and nothing in others — and an identifiable texture is a worse failure than flatness, because
it announces itself and stops being material at all.

## §1 — Identity

Surface is the discipline of **what a field is made of**.

It owns one property: the material character of every surface in the experience, and the rule by which
a surface either has material or does not. It exists independently because material is neither colour,
nor space, nor depth, nor content, and each of those disciplines would reasonably decline it.

Without it, surfaces are flat by default and textured by preference — which is the same as ungoverned.
The absence is invisible to every other discipline, because a flat surface is not incorrect in any
respect any of them can measure.

## §2 — Mission

Surface exists so that **every field in the experience feels like something made, and the reader never
notices why.**

Its long-term contribution is a tactile quality that survives any palette, any layout and any asset —
a consistent sense that the experience has the character of printed matter rather than of a display.
Success is a reader who perceives a surface as substantial and could not name a texture if asked.

## §3 — Constitutional Responsibility

This discipline owns **material intent**: whether a surface carries material, what kind, and how far
the surface recedes behind what it carries.

**What it inherits.** The felt register, from Editorial Art Direction — whether the moment should feel
handled or clean. The spatial allocation, from Section Layout. The Experience Character, from Brand
Expression. Which surfaces exist at all, from the Component System.

**What it contributes.** A rule for which categories of surface carry material and why, and the
governed relationship between a surface and the content it holds.

**What it rejects.** It does not choose the surface's colour. It does not create depth — elevation,
shadow, glow and blur are not material and are not this discipline's to introduce by another name. It
does not decide what sits on a surface. It does not treat typography: type is never inside the
composite, and a discipline that lets material reach the glyphs has stopped governing surfaces and
started degrading text.

## §4 — Core Question

> **What is this surface made of, and why is it made of that rather than nothing?**

The second half is the discipline's whole difficulty. Material that cannot answer *why* is material
applied because it was available, and that is the failure mode this discipline most reliably produces.

## §5 — Inputs

- **The felt register** (Editorial Art Direction) — whether the moment wants to feel handled or clean.
- **The Spatial Model** — how much field there is, and whether it is a band or a panel.
- **The Mechanism Model** — which surfaces the composition actually contains.
- **The Experience Character** — the enduring identity material must remain an expression of.

## §6 — Transformation

> **A surface that exists → a surface that is made of something.**

What enters is a field with a role and an extent. What is applied is the judgment of whether that
field's *kind* warrants material. What emerges is material intent: a surface that is now a substance
rather than a computed colour.

## §7 — Outputs

- **Material intent per surface category** — which kinds of surface carry material, and which do not.
- **The recession relationship** — how far a surface stands behind its content.
- **Reasoning** — why a category qualifies, available to the rulebook that operationalizes it.

It outputs no texture, no asset, no value and no colour.

## §8 — Discipline Knowledge

### Material is a property of a surface, not a second surface

The most consequential idea in the discipline, and the easiest to lose. Material is what a surface is
made *of* — not a layer placed *on* it. A treatment understood as a layer immediately acquires the
properties of a layer: it can be positioned, it can be varied, it can be applied to part of a field,
and it begins to mark and separate. At that point it has become a decorative element that happens to
be textured, and the surface underneath is as flat as it ever was.

The consequence is that material is **uniform across a surface by necessity, not by convention**. It
marks nothing, separates nothing, and indicates nothing. The moment a reader can locate material — can
say it is stronger here than there — it has stopped being material.

### Material is not depth, and the distinction is not pedantic

Both make a flat field feel less flat, which is why they are confused. They do it by opposite means.
Depth simulates a third dimension: it lifts something above something else and creates a hierarchy of
elevation. Material adds substance *within* the plane: it says what the plane is made of and lifts
nothing.

A system that forbids depth and permits material is not inconsistent. It has drawn the line exactly
where perception draws it — a surface may have substance without pretending to be somewhere in space.
A treatment that begins to imply elevation has crossed from one to the other and must be read as depth
regardless of what produced it.

### A surface is perceived as an environment before it is read as a layout

A large field is taken in whole before its contents are resolved. This ordering is a fact about
perception, and it has one important corollary that is regularly misread: **perception order is not
visual dominance.** That a surface is perceived first does not entitle it to compete with what it
carries. It establishes the atmosphere the content is then met within, and then it recedes.

Recession is therefore not the absence of material but its correct amount. A surface that asserts
itself has not been made more substantial; it has been made into content.

### Material belongs to a category of surface, never to an instance

If material is applied because someone chose to apply it here, then it varies by author, and the
uniformity that makes it material is lost across the experience even where it holds within a single
field. The rule that survives is categorical: a surface carries material **because of what it is**,
and no author-facing choice exists at the call site.

This is also what makes the discipline verifiable. "Material appears on exactly these categories and
nowhere else" is checkable; "material appears where it looks right" is not.

### The floor is not zero

A discipline that governs material by restricting it will drift toward applying it nowhere, because
every individual restriction is defensible and the cumulative result is never examined. An experience
in which material is technically defined but reaches almost nothing is not restrained — it is flat,
and it has the governance of tactility without the tactility.

The correct question is not *may this surface carry material?* but *what kind of surface is this, and
does that kind carry material?* — asked of every category the system has, not only of the ones already
answered.

## §9 — Organizational Design System Decisions

- **This system treats material as compositional rather than authored.** No author-facing switch
  exists; a surface's material follows from its category. Adopted because a handle at the call site
  makes uniformity a matter of discipline rather than of mechanism.
- **Material composes from a fixed, shared set applied identically wherever it appears.** One
  vocabulary, not a palette of textures. Adopted so a reader perceives a tactile surface and never an
  identifiable texture.
- **A photographic base takes an additional tonal layer that a solid colour does not.** Adopted
  because the two are different substances: a photograph already carries its own grain and light, and
  treating it identically to flat colour ignores what it is.
- **Typography is never inside the composite, and this is enforced rather than intended.** Adopted
  because material reaching the glyphs degrades legibility invisibly — it survives every review that
  looks at declared styles.
- **Material is suppressed in print and in forced-colours mode.** Adopted because both are conditions
  nobody looks at, and a rule that silently stops applying is worse than no rule.

## §10 — Primary Primitives

**Material — what the surface is made of.** The substance itself: whether a field is a manufactured
flat colour or something with tooth. Every other decision in the discipline is a decision about this
one's presence, kind or degree.

**Uniformity — the evenness of that substance across the field.** Not a quality of material but a
*condition* of it: material that varies within a surface has become an element. Uniformity is what
separates material from decoration, and it is the primitive most often violated by someone trying to
be expressive.

**Recession — how far the surface stands behind what it carries.** A surface establishes atmosphere
and then yields. Recession governs the amount it yields by, and it is what keeps a substantial surface
from becoming a competing one.

The three are dependent in one direction: material without uniformity is decoration, and material
without recession is content. Uniformity and recession are not additional features of material — they
are the conditions under which material remains material at all.

## §11 — Decision Intelligence

**Ask what kind of surface this is, never whether this surface would look better with material.** The
first question produces a categorical answer that holds across the system; the second produces a
per-instance preference that destroys uniformity while appearing to improve each case.

**Let the register say whether the moment wants handling.** Editorial Art Direction has already decided
whether a moment should feel worked or clean. Material is one of the instruments that realizes that,
and it should be reasoned from the register rather than from the surface's appearance.

**When a treatment starts to imply elevation, read it as depth and stop.** The test is not what
produced the effect but what a reader perceives. Material that lifts is depth wearing a texture.

**Treat "the reader can name the texture" as a failure, not a success.** If material is noticeable as
an identifiable pattern, it has become an element. The correct amount is the amount at which a reader
perceives substance and reports nothing.

**Prefer extending a category over exempting an instance.** An exception at a call site is a handle,
and a handle is how uniformity is lost. If a surface genuinely should not carry material, the category
is wrong and should be corrected.

## §12 — Behaviour

The discipline behaves like a printer rather than a decorator: concerned with what the stock is,
indifferent to what is printed on it, and confident that the best result is one nobody remarks on. It
is systematic — it reasons about categories, not cases. It is restrained without being absent, and it
treats the temptation to make material visible as the discipline's characteristic error rather than as
enthusiasm.

## §13 — Cross-Discipline Relationships

- **Editorial Art Direction, consulted before.** Tells this discipline whether a moment should feel
  handled or clean. It never specifies material; this discipline never re-decides register.
- **Colour.** Decides what a surface *is* chromatically; this discipline decides what it is made of.
  The two are independent, and either can change without the other.
- **Illustration.** Owns a creative as a *subject*. Where a creative is used as a *surface*, this
  discipline governs its material and its recession. The seam is the creative's job, not its file.
- **Typography.** Its glyphs are never inside the composite. This is a one-way protection this
  discipline owes Typography, not a negotiation between them.
- **Section Layout, above.** Decides how much field exists. This discipline never asks for more.
- **`rulebooks/visual-language.md` §1 and §1.1, below.** Operationalize this expertise into the
  enabled categories and the composition order. They inherit this reasoning and never author it.

## §14 — Quality Intelligence

- Does this surface have material because of what it *is*, or because someone applied it?
- Could a reader locate the material — say it is stronger here than there? If so, it is an element.
- Does the surface recede behind its content, or compete with it?
- Is there any category of surface in this system whose material question has never been asked?
- Does anything here simulate elevation under another name?
- Would this hold if the texture assets were replaced with entirely different ones?

## §15 — Failure Modes

**Material as decoration.** Treatment applied per-instance for effect. Uniformity is lost, and the
surface becomes an element that happens to be textured.

**Depth by another name.** A treatment that lifts. Forbidden depth reintroduced through the one
discipline permitted to make a surface feel less flat.

**Identifiable texture.** Material strong enough to be named. It has stopped being substance and
become a pattern.

**Flat-by-omission.** Material defined but reaching almost nothing, so the experience is flat while
appearing governed. The system has the rule and not the quality.

**The author-facing handle.** A switch at the call site. Uniformity becomes a matter of discipline
rather than mechanism, and it is lost the first time someone is in a hurry.

**Material on type.** The composite reaches the glyphs. Legibility degrades in a way no review of
declared styles can see.

**Silent suspension.** Material that stops applying in print or forced-colours without anyone
noticing, so the rule holds only in the conditions people happen to look at.

## §16 — Verification

**Categorical fidelity.** Material appears on exactly the enabled categories and nowhere else —
checkable, and checked.

**Uniformity.** Material does not vary within a surface.

**Isolation.** Glyphs are provably outside the composite, measured rather than asserted.

**Recession.** Content remains dominant over the surface carrying it.

**Suspension.** Material is absent in print and forced colours, asserted rather than intended.

**Coverage.** Every surface category in the system has an answer, including "no material" — an
unanswered category is a gap, not a default.

## §17 — Evolution

The discipline matures by understanding substance better, not by acquiring more textures. A larger
texture vocabulary is a weaker one: it reintroduces choice, and choice reintroduces the
per-instance application that uniformity cannot survive.

It grows by asking the material question of categories it has not yet asked it of, by getting more
precise about recession, and by learning to detect depth arriving under new names. It does not grow by
following surface trends, which change what a texture looks like and nothing about why a surface needs
one.
