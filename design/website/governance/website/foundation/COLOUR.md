# COLOUR — The Design Discipline of Where Colour Is Spent

> **What this is.** The Foundation discipline that owns **colour as a finite resource and where it is
> spent.** It decides what colour is *for* in this experience, how much of it any moment may carry,
> and what a reader should understand from meeting it. Its output is not a palette, a hex value or a
> token; it is **chromatic intent**.
>
> **What this is not.** Not the palette itself — which hues this brand owns is inherited from Brand
> Expression and is never re-authored here. Not surface material, which is what a field is made of
> rather than what colour it is. Not typography, spacing or layout. It names no value.
>
> **The one boundary above all others.** *Colour determines what colour means in this experience and
> where it is spent. It never invents a hue, never decides what a surface is made of, and never
> decides what any element is or where it sits. It governs chromatic intent, and nothing else.*
>
> **The test every line passes.** *Would this still be true if the brand's hues were replaced
> tomorrow, while colour still did the same job?*
>
> **Where this sits.** A Design Discipline of the Website Foundation, reasoned through after
> `EDITORIAL_ART_DIRECTION.md` — which tells it the emotional temperature a moment must carry.
> **On any conflict, Governance wins, and this document wins over `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §15,
> which operationalizes it.**

---

## §0 — First principles

Colour is the only visual property that cannot be used a little without being used deliberately. Space
can be approximate. Type can be a step off and still read. Colour is either doing something or is
noise, because the eye finds it before anything else and assigns it meaning whether or not meaning was
intended.

That priority is the discipline's whole problem. Because colour is found first, it is the fastest way
to direct attention — and for the same reason it is the fastest way to destroy a hierarchy. Two
coloured things on a field compete regardless of what they mean; three make a pattern the reader will
try to interpret. Colour used without a rule does not fail quietly. It actively misinforms.

The naïve governance response is a ceiling: limit colour, and it cannot do harm. This is where a
system arrives at the failure that produces genuinely neutral work. A ceiling forbids excess and
mandates nothing, so the safest possible page — the one with no colour at all — passes every check.
Restraint enforced only from above is indistinguishable from absence, and a system with a ceiling and
no floor will drift to grey and report itself compliant.

The discipline therefore exists to govern colour in both directions: what it is *for*, so that it
appears where it is doing work, and how much may appear, so that it keeps meaning something when it
does. Neither half is sufficient alone. The ceiling without the floor produces neutrality; the floor
without the ceiling produces noise.

## §1 — Identity

Colour is the discipline of **where colour is spent**.

It owns the meaning colour carries in this experience, the hierarchy among its accents, and the amount
any moment may hold. It exists independently because the question *what should be coloured, and why*
is answered by nothing else: Brand Expression supplies which hues exist, Visual Language decides what
must be perceptible, and neither decides where colour goes.

Without it, colour is applied by preference within a ceiling — which produces pages that are
technically compliant and chromatically inert, and no discipline is positioned to notice, because
nothing was violated.

## §2 — Mission

Colour exists so that **every use of colour directs attention to something that deserved it, and the
experience is never neutral by omission.**

Success is a reader whose eye is taken to what mattered, in an experience that could not be mistaken
for a greyscale one, using no more colour than the argument earned.

## §3 — Constitutional Responsibility

This discipline owns **chromatic intent**: what colour means here, the hierarchy among accents, and
the amount permitted per moment.

**What it inherits.** The palette, from Brand Expression — which hues this brand owns is settled, and
this discipline never adds one. The emotional temperature of a moment, from Editorial Art Direction.
What must be perceptible and in what order, from Visual Language.

**What it contributes.** A rule for what colour is *for*, an ordered hierarchy among the accents, and
the bounds on how much colour a moment may carry.

**What it rejects.** It does not author a hue. It does not decide what a surface is made of. It does
not decide what an element is, where it sits, or how large it is. And it does not own contrast as a
legibility requirement — that is a floor imposed from Visual Language and accessibility, which this
discipline must satisfy and may never trade against.

## §4 — Core Question

> **What is this colour doing, and what would be lost if it were absent?**

The second half is what separates the discipline from decoration. Colour that survives its own removal
without loss was decoration; colour whose absence costs the reader something was spent well.

## §5 — Inputs

- **The palette** (Brand Expression) — the hues this experience owns. Fixed.
- **The felt register** (Editorial Art Direction) — the emotional temperature the moment must carry.
- **The Visual Model** — what must be perceptible, and the order of perception.
- **The Spatial Model** — what the moment's parts are, so colour can be spent on one of them.

## §6 — Transformation

> **A palette and a moment → a decision about where colour is spent.**

What enters is a fixed set of hues and a moment with a hierarchy. What is applied is the judgment of
which single thing in that moment most deserves to be found first. What emerges is chromatic intent.

## §7 — Outputs

- **The accent hierarchy** — an ordered priority among the accents, so reaching for one is a decision
  with a default rather than a preference.
- **Chromatic allocation per moment** — how much colour a moment carries, and on what.
- **Reasoning** — why colour appears where it does.

It outputs no hue, no token and no value.

## §8 — Discipline Knowledge

### Colour is found before it is read

The eye locates chromatic difference pre-attentively — before reading, before deciding to look. This
is why colour is the strongest available instrument for directing attention and the most destructive
when unmanaged: it does not wait to be interpreted.

The practical consequence is that **the number of coloured things in a field matters more than which
colours they are.** One coloured element in a neutral field is a direction. Three are a pattern, and a
reader will spend effort deciding what the pattern means, which is effort taken from the content.

### An accent is a spark, not a wash

An accent works by scarcity. Its power is entirely a function of how little of it there is, which
means the instrument destroys itself under use: colour applied generously stops being an accent and
becomes a background, and the field it was meant to direct attention within becomes the thing it is.

This is why the discipline reasons about accents in terms of *spend* rather than *choice*. The
question is never which accent to use but whether this moment has earned any.

### Restraint is not neutrality, and a ceiling alone cannot tell them apart

The distinction the discipline lives on. **Restraint** is colour withheld here so it lands harder
there — a decision with a purpose and a beneficiary. **Neutrality** is colour never decided. They look
identical in any single moment and are opposite in a sequence: restraint produces a page with peaks,
neutrality produces a page without any.

A ceiling cannot distinguish them, because both satisfy it. Only a rule that asks what colour is *for*
can, which is why this discipline governs a floor as well.

### A hierarchy among accents removes a decision that should not be made repeatedly

Where several accents are equally available, each use becomes a fresh choice, and fresh choices vary.
The result is a system with a consistent palette and an inconsistent language — every page correct,
no two alike in what a colour means.

An ordered hierarchy fixes this cheaply: there is a first reach, a second, and a rest, and reaching
past one requires a reason. This does not reduce expressiveness. It relocates the decision from every
call site to one place, which is the only form restraint can take at scale.

### Colour carries temperature independently of intensity

A moment can be quiet and warm, or forceful and cold. Reading these as one axis — treating "more
colour" as "more feeling" — collapses the discipline's usefulness to a volume knob. Temperature is
which hues are spent; intensity is how much. Editorial Art Direction supplies both as separate
requirements, and this discipline must keep them separate.

### Legibility is a floor, never a trade

Contrast requirements are not this discipline's to balance against expression. They are a condition it
operates within. A colour decision that improves a composition and fails contrast has not made a
trade-off; it has produced text some readers cannot read, which is not a design outcome that competes
with other design outcomes.

## §9 — Organizational Design System Decisions

- **This system reaches for its accents in a fixed order.** The identity's own marker colour first,
  then the secondary accent, then the remainder. Adopted so that reaching past the first requires a
  reason and consistency does not depend on memory.
- **A moment carries one *dominant* accent, and few moments.** Adopted because a second accent of
  equal weight in one field creates a pattern the reader must interpret, while a deliberate set led by
  one accent does not. Density and area are separate limits and neither substitutes for the other: a
  handful of tiny marks in five different accents satisfies an area budget and is exactly the
  scattering this rule exists to stop. *(Resolved — `DECISION-REGISTER.md` DR-1.)*
- **Consecutive marked moments do not share an accent.** Adopted because two marks close together
  read as one relationship, and colour is what tells a reader which two belong together.
- **Colour is never the sole carrier of meaning.** Adopted as an accessibility floor; a reader who
  cannot distinguish two hues must lose nothing.
- **The palette is inherited and closed.** No hue is authored here. Adopted so a brand swap re-themes
  the experience without reopening this discipline.
- **Colour never establishes a component's identity.** Adopted because a component is defined by its
  semantic role, its hierarchy and its interaction — never by its fill. A rule of the form "this
  component is that colour" makes the colour load-bearing, and the moment it is load-bearing it can no
  longer be spent, withheld, or varied for communicative reasons. **Colour reinforces hierarchy; it
  never creates it.** Hierarchy is made by composition, typography, spacing and position, and colour
  confirms what those already said.

### The role table — what each inherited accent is for

Every accent this system inherits has **one job**, and the discipline's spend decisions are made
against the job rather than the hue. The table is stated in **roles**, not hues: which hue fills which
role is a Brand-Pack value, and this discipline would say the same thing if every hue changed
tomorrow.

| Role | What it is for | How often it should appear |
|---|---|---|
| **The canvas** | the architectural ground the experience is built on | dominant — the large majority of every page |
| **The raised neutral** | elevation; content lifted off the canvas | frequent |
| **The emphasis accent** | editorial emphasis — a moment given deliberate additional attention | rare |
| **The information accent** | information, orientation, and environmental grounding at scale | moderate |
| **The intelligence accent** | intelligence and premium capability | rare |
| **The positive accent** | success, validation, completion | rare |

Two things this table is careful *not* to be. It is not a component map — no row names a button, a
link, or a badge, because that would be the identity failure above. And "how often" is a frequency of
*occasion*, not a quota: an accent that appears because its occasion arose is correct even if it
appears rarely, and an accent distributed to meet a target has stopped meaning anything.

*(The frequencies are given as words here deliberately. The measurable shares and per-section ceilings
belong downstream, where a number can be checked — see `rulebooks/visual-language.md` §15 and
`component-governance/COMPOSE.md` §C.5.)*

## §10 — Primary Primitives

**Hue — which colour it is.** The identity-carrying primitive and the one that determines temperature.
Hue is inherited rather than chosen, and this discipline reasons about which inherited hue is spent,
never about inventing one.

**Lightness — how light or dark it is.** The primitive that does almost all of the legibility work and
most of the hierarchy work. Lightness relationships are what make text readable and what let a field
recede behind its content, and they operate whether or not a reader perceives hue at all — which is
why lightness, not hue, is where accessibility is won or lost.

**Chroma — how much colour is present.** The primitive of spend. Chroma is what makes an accent an
accent, what a ceiling limits, and what a floor requires. A system reasoning only about hue has no
vocabulary for *how much*, which is the question that actually governs restraint.

*(The term "lightness" is used deliberately in place of "value", which throughout this stack means a
token value.)*

The three are independent, and the discipline's judgment lives in holding them apart: temperature is a
hue decision, hierarchy is a lightness decision, and restraint is a chroma decision. Collapsing them
into "more colour" is how the discipline degrades into a volume control.

## §11 — Decision Intelligence

**Ask what the eye should find first, then colour that and nothing else.** Colour is an attention
instrument before it is an aesthetic one, and a moment with one intended destination has one candidate
for colour.

**Reach in the established order, and require a reason to reach past.** Not because the first accent
is best, but because a default removes a decision that would otherwise be made differently each time.

**Read the register for temperature and the argument for spend — separately.** Editorial Art Direction
supplies both, and conflating them produces a page where every warm moment is also a loud one.

**When in doubt, spend nothing here.** The asymmetry is real: colour withheld can be added, and colour
spent has already cost the next moment its impact. But apply this per moment, never as a policy —
applied as policy it is the neutrality failure, and the sequence must still have peaks.

**Treat contrast as a boundary, not an input.** It does not participate in the trade-off; it defines
the space the trade-off happens inside.

**Check the sequence, not the moment.** A page where no single moment violates the ceiling and no
moment carries any colour has failed this discipline completely while passing every local check.

### When two of these collide — the order of authority

Most chromatic decisions are made by one consideration. The hard ones are made by two that disagree,
and without a stated order each gets decided differently every time. For colour, the order is:

> **Accessibility → Semantic correctness → Approved reference fidelity → Visual preference.**

The higher tier wins, and the reasoning behind the sequence is the sequence's justification:

- **Accessibility is first because it is a boundary, not an input** — the position §8 already takes.
  A decision that reads better and cannot be read by some readers has not won a trade-off.
- **Semantic correctness outranks fidelity** because a reference shows *one* correct composition, and
  a role reproduced with the wrong meaning is wrong everywhere else it recurs.
- **Fidelity outranks preference** because the approved work is evidence and preference is assertion.
- **Preference is last, and it is still on the list** — it decides everything the tiers above leave
  genuinely open, which is most decisions.

**"Approved" is doing real work in the third tier.** It admits only the canonical approved references.
Without that word, any image anyone found becomes an authority, "the reference does it" stops being a
checkable claim, and the tier quietly becomes preference wearing evidence's clothes.

*(This precedence is stated here for chromatic decisions because this is the discipline that needed it
first. It very likely generalizes; promoting it is a deliberate decision for the tier above, not
something to assume from this paragraph. See `DECISION-REGISTER.md` DR-33.)*

## §12 — Behaviour

The discipline behaves like an editor with a fixed budget: aware of the whole before spending on the
part, comfortable saying no to a good use because a better one is coming, and unimpressed by colour
for its own sake. It is decisive — a moment either earns colour or does not — and it is suspicious of
its own generosity, because the failure it produces under pressure is always more.

## §13 — Cross-Discipline Relationships

- **Editorial Art Direction, consulted before.** Supplies emotional temperature and intensity as
  separate requirements. This discipline realizes them; it never re-decides register.
- **Surface.** Decides what a field is made of; this discipline decides what colour it is. Independent
  — either can change without the other.
- **Typography.** Consumes lightness relationships for legibility. This discipline owes Typography a
  contrast floor it may never trade against.
- **Iconography.** An icon may carry an accent; whether it may is this discipline's, what form it
  takes is Iconography's.
- **Illustration.** A creative carries its own colour, which this discipline does not govern and never
  modifies. Where a creative sits behind text, the contrast floor still applies.
- **Brand Expression, above.** Supplies the palette. Never re-authored here.
- **`rulebooks/visual-language.md` §15, below.** Operationalizes this expertise into roles and the
  reach order. It inherits this reasoning and never authors it.

## §14 — Quality Intelligence

- What is this colour doing? What is lost if it is removed?
- Did the eye go where the argument wanted it to?
- Is this restraint, or is it neutrality? Where in this experience does colour actually land?
- Was this accent reached for in order, and if not, what was the reason?
- Does anything here depend on colour alone to be understood?
- Would a reader who cannot distinguish these two hues lose anything?
- Across the whole sequence, does colour have peaks — or only an absence of violations?

## §15 — Failure Modes

**Neutrality by ceiling.** A system that only limits colour drifts to none and reports compliance. The
characteristic failure of governing colour from one side.

**Accent inflation.** Colour applied generously until it is a background. The instrument consumed by
its own use.

**Decorative colour.** Colour that survives its own removal without loss. It was never doing work.

**Fresh choice at every call site.** No hierarchy, so each use is decided independently. A consistent
palette with an inconsistent language.

**Temperature-as-volume.** Warmth and intensity collapsed into one axis, so every warm moment is loud
and every quiet one is cold.

**Contrast traded for composition.** A legibility floor treated as one consideration among several.
The output is text some readers cannot read.

**Colour as the only signal.** Meaning carried by hue alone, invisible to a portion of readers.

## §16 — Verification

**Ceiling — two limits, and neither implies the other.** No moment exceeds its permitted accent
**count**, and no moment exceeds its permitted accent **area**. Density and area fail in opposite
directions: five tiny marks in five accents clear any area budget and are pure scattering, while three
marks in one accent covering a third of a band satisfy the count and swamp the moment. A system holding
only one of these limits reports compliance it has not measured.

**Floor.** Colour actually lands somewhere across the experience. A page with no accent anywhere is a
failure this discipline must be able to state, or the ceiling is the only rule it has.

**Role legality.** No token is used in a role it does not measure for — an accent that fails contrast
as text is never text, whatever its hue makes it look suited to.

**Identity.** No component's colour is the thing that makes it that component.

**Order.** Accents are reached for in the established priority; departures are deliberate.

**Contrast.** Every text node clears its floor against what is actually rendered behind it, measured
rather than declared.

**Independence.** Nothing depends on hue alone to be understood.

**Sequence.** Consecutive marked moments do not share an accent.

## §17 — Evolution

The discipline matures by getting better at *not* spending, and by getting more precise about what a
colour was for — not by acquiring more hues. A larger palette is a weaker one: it multiplies the
decisions that a hierarchy exists to remove.

It grows by learning where colour genuinely changed what a reader did, by tightening the floor so
neutrality becomes as visible a failure as noise, and by understanding perception more deeply. It does
not grow by adopting current palettes, which change which hues are fashionable and nothing about how
attention works.
