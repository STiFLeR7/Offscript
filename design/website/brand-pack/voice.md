# voice.md — Brand Voice (Brand-Pack value layer)

> currently-installed brand, paired with the *why* behind each rule (Value + Why). It is the
> swappable layer: replace this file when the brand changes. Governance (the rulebooks, the
> philosophy) references "the brand voice" by role and never restates these specifics.

## The voice in one line
**Decisive, outcome-numeric, lightly confrontational** — written for VP-of-Operations and CFO
readers who have already been sold a lot of bad AI.
**Why:** the audience is skeptical and senior; the voice earns trust by leaning on *measurable
consequences* and on *what we won't do*, not on enthusiasm.

## Person — who speaks, who is spoken to
- Speak **to "you"** (the buyer's organisation) and **from "we"** (the brand). Never first-person
  singular. Never "users."
- ✅ "We deploy parallel to your current workflow." · ✅ "Your best people spend most of their day
  on work that requires no judgment." · ❌ "I will help you" / "The user can…"
- **Why:** first-person-plural + second-person keeps the relationship a partnership between two
  organisations; "users" makes the reader a statistic.

## Headlines
- **Title Case**, with a strong period or rhythm break.
- e.g. "Turn Manual Ops Into Autonomous Profit Engines, In Weeks." · "Every Workflow Running. Every
  Exception Caught. Every Decision Backed By Data." · "Your People Are Ready. Your Systems Are
  Holding Them Back."
- **Why:** the period and the rhythm break give a headline the cadence of a stated fact, which
  reads as confidence rather than hype.

## Body
- Plainspoken **sentence case**, often built from short declarative bursts strung with line breaks
  instead of commas: *"No backlogs. / No blind spots. / No added headcount."*
- **Why:** short bursts are scannable and land like a list of guarantees; the reader gets the gist
  without reading a paragraph.

## Numbers as proof
- Stat figures are **the argument, not decoration.** Always anchor a claim to a percentage,
  multiple, count, or timeframe (e.g. 92% cost removal, 11.3× ROI, 80% routine work automated,
  3-day payback, 4-week pilots, 5 FTE automated, 30–50% cycle-time reduction).
- **Why:** a senior, skeptical reader believes a specific number and discounts a vague superlative;
  specificity is the credibility mechanism.

## CTAs
- **Imperative, second-person, ending in a "→" arrow** on a single line: "Book a Demo Call →" ·
  "Show for My Ops →" · "Get My No-Cost Process Audit →" · "See Case Studies".
- Followed by a small, grey **reassurance line**: *"30 minutes · No commitment · We show you the
  numbers first."*
- **Why:** the imperative makes the next step obvious; the reassurance line lowers the perceived
  cost of saying yes at the exact moment of the ask.

## Comparison framing
- When juxtaposing the brand vs. the status quo, the **competitor column is neutral cool grey** and
  the **brand column carries a coloured accent** — never insult the other tool; let the contrast do
  the work.
- **Why:** punching down reads as insecurity; a calm contrast reads as confidence.

## FAQ pattern
- Phrase questions **the way a CFO would actually ask them** ("Is our data safe?", "What happens if
  the agent makes a mistake?") and answer **concretely**, naming the specific systems integrated
  with by brand.
- **Why:** concrete, system-named answers meet the objection where the buyer forms it (Trust
  Philosophy: address doubt at the moment it arises).

## What the voice bans
- **No emoji. No exclamation points.**
- **Banned words:** "powerful", "robust", "leverage", "synergy", "revolutionary", "AI-native"
  (except as a *destination* phrase the buyer is moving toward).
- **No** "we believe" / "our mission is" / brand-mission filler.
- **No** tech-jargon ladders ("LLM-driven, vector-indexed…") on marketing surfaces — the argument is
  *outcomes in your stack*, not architecture.
- **Why:** every banned item is a credibility leak — it either signals hype, hand-waving, or
  inward-facing language to a reader who is buying outcomes.
